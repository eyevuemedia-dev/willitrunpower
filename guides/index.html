How runtimes on these pages are calculated

Every runtime uses the same published rule from our methodology: runtime = nameplate Wh × 0.85 ÷ (average load W + about 15 W). The 0.85 is our allowance for inverter loss, not a measured efficiency for any model. The 15 W is inverter self-consumption, the figure BLUETTI uses in its AC180 runtime formula; it barely matters for a 1,000 W heater but cuts a router or compact-fridge estimate by a third or more. CPAP pages also show DC-converter nights separately as a best case.

For starting a motor (fridge, sump pump, AC), a station only counts its surge figure when the maker lists it separately from a heater-only boost mode. X-Boost, Power Lifting and SurgePad are treated as resistive-only.

Generator rules every guide uses

Running watts is what a generator holds continuously; starting watts is the brief burst for a motor start. A generator clears an appliance only when running draw fits running watts and startup draw fits starting watts. When a maker does not publish startup watts, we require starting watts of at least 3× running draw and say so. Dual-fuel models are compared on their gasoline rating. Details are in the methodology.

Safety: run generators outdoors only, at least 20 feet from the home with exhaust pointed away from doors, windows and vents; use CO alarms; never backfeed through a wall outlet or dryer plug; and have a licensed electrician install a transfer switch or listed interlock. See generator safety.
