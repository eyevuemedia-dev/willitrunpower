The 8 quietest inverter generators of 2026, ranked by published dBA

By the Willrun team · Updated November 26, 2026 · Prices checked November 21, 2026

The quietest inverter generator you can buy new is the Honda EU2200i: Honda publishes 48 dB(A) at quarter load and 57 at rated load, it weighs 47.4 lb and shuts itself off if CO builds up. If $1,199 is too much, the Champion 200951 is rated 53 dBA from 23 ft at about half the price and 39 lb. For an RV with a 30 A plug the Predator 3500 (56 dBA, $799.99, CO shutdown) is the value pick, and for 240 V home backup through an inlet the Honda EU7000iS (52–58 dB(A)) is the quiet one. If you need truly silent power for a CPAP or a few hours of lights, skip the engine and use a power station.

You should get…

  • the Honda EU2200i if you camp, tailgate or run a fridge, lights and phones near neighbors and want the lowest published figure: 48 dB(A) at 1/4 load ($1,199 MSRP).
  • the Champion 2500W Ultralight (200951) if you want a sub-55 dBA figure (53 dBA from 23 ft) for about half the Honda's price and can lift 39 lb into a trunk.
  • the Honda EU3000iS if you run a 13,500 BTU RV roof AC in campgrounds with quiet hours.
  • the Predator 3500 Super Quiet if you want 3,000 W, a 30 A RV outlet and CO shutdown for about a third of the EU3000iS price ($799.99 on Nov 21).
  • the Honda EU7000iS if you need 240 V for a well pump or furnace through a 30 A inlet and the neighbors are close.
  • a power station instead if the job is a CPAP or a few hours of lights and you need it silent.

How we chose (and what we did not do)

We did not run these generators or measure them ourselves. Every noise figure on this page is the number the maker publishes, on the maker's product page or manual, and every price was read on the maker's or a retailer's page on November 21, 2026. We ranked inverter generators by their lowest published A-weighted figure, then picked one model for each common job: camping, an RV with a 30 A plug, dual fuel, and 240 V home backup through a 30 A inlet. Models without a published dBA figure are left out rather than guessed. Where makers measure differently (load, distance), we say so, and we treat gaps of 2–3 dB as ties.

How we ranked, heaviest criterion first

  1. Lowest published A-weighted noise, at the lightest load the maker states. Gaps of 2–3 dB count as ties, and models with no published figure are left out.
  2. Fit for the job: running watts and the outlet the job needs (30 A RV, 240 V L14-30R, dual fuel).
  3. Safety: a published CO shutoff.
  4. Price on the maker's or a retailer's page on November 21, 2026.
  5. Weight and how easy it is to move.

No numeric weights: a higher criterion decides first, and a lower one only breaks ties within a job.

What the dB numbers actually mean

Decibels are logarithmic. Every +10 dB is ten times the sound energy and, to most listeners, roughly twice as loud (see HyperPhysics on loudness). So the 9 dB spread between the EU2200i at quarter load (48) and at rated load (57) is close to a doubling, and the 15 dB gap between the EU7000iS and the Predator 9500 on paper is close to three times as loud.

  • Load matters more than brand. Honda is one of the few makers to publish both ends: 48→57 dB(A) on the EU2200i, 50→57 on the EU3000iS, 52→58 on the EU7000iS. A single "52 dBA" with no load stated is almost always the light-load, eco-mode number.
  • Distance is rarely stated. Champion says 23 feet (7 m) for both of its units. The other spec pages we used give no distance, so small differences between brands are not meaningful. We treat 2–3 dB as a tie.
  • Two in parallel add about 3 dB. Two equal sources double the energy, which is +3 dB, so a pair of EU2200i units at quarter load is roughly 51 dB(A), still quieter than one Predator 3500 on paper.
  • Tone matters too. A-weighting tracks how loud a sound seems, not how annoying a low exhaust drone is. Inverters throttle down at light load, which is most of the difference from open-frame units that run at a fixed speed.

How loud is each one at 50 and 100 feet?

Campgrounds and HOAs care about noise at your neighbor, not at the spec-sheet distance. National Park Service rules (36 CFR 2.12) prohibit running a generator louder than 60 dB(A) measured at 50 feet, or in an unreasonable way even if it is under that level, and many campgrounds add quiet hours. Make any generator quieter (safely)

  1. Distance first. Doubling the distance takes about 6 dB off. Use a heavier-gauge cord so you can put the unit farther away; never closer than 20 feet to the house, with exhaust pointed away from doors, windows and vents (see the safety box below and the CDC on carbon monoxide).
  2. Point the exhaust away from you and your neighbors. Most of the noise leaves through the muffler side.
  3. Use eco mode and keep the load light. That is the difference between the quarter-load and rated-load figures, up to 9 dB on the Hondas.
  4. Put something solid in the line of sight, such as a vehicle, a woodpile or a berm, with open air all around the unit. Never box a generator in: enclosures that aren't made for your model can trap CO and overheat it.
  5. Run it on soft ground, not a hard deck or truck bed that resonates.
  6. Run it less. Charge a power station for a few hours, then shut the generator off for the night.

Safety first

  • Run a generator outdoors only, at least 20 feet from the house, with the exhaust pointed away from doors, windows and vents. Never in a garage, basement, shed or crawlspace, even with the door open.
  • Carbon monoxide is odorless and can kill in minutes. Keep battery CO alarms working inside the home.
  • Never backfeed a house by plugging a generator into a wall outlet or dryer receptacle. It can energize utility lines and kill a lineworker or a neighbor.
  • Connecting to home wiring requires a transfer switch or a listed breaker interlock with a power inlet box, installed by a licensed electrician and permitted where your jurisdiction requires it.

Questions

What is the quietest inverter generator?

Among models with a published figure, the Honda EU2200i: 48 dB(A) at quarter load and 57 dB(A) at rated load, per Honda. The EU3000iS (50 dB(A)) is the quietest with a 30 A outlet, and the EU7000iS (52 dB(A)) is the quietest 240 V unit in our data.

Is a 52 dB generator quiet?

Yes, for a generator. Honda and Westinghouse both describe their low-50s figures as quieter than normal conversation. Projected to 50 feet it is roughly 45 dB(A), well under the 60 dB(A) limit the National Park Service applies at 50 feet. Check what load and distance the 52 refers to, though: it is usually the eco-mode, light-load number.

How many decibels is a quiet generator at 50 feet?

Using the inverse-square rule and assuming the published figure is at 23 feet, a 48 dB(A) unit is about 41 dB(A) at 50 feet, a 56 dBA unit about 49, and a 67 dBA unit about 60. Real yards with walls and hard ground are louder than this best case.

Are inverter generators quieter than regular generators?

Usually, because the engine throttles down at light load and the unit sits in a sound-damped case. But not always: the Predator 9500 is an inverter rated 67 dBA at 25% load. Compare published figures, not the word inverter.

Can I run two quiet generators in parallel without much more noise?

Two identical units add about 3 dB, not double the decibels. Two EU2200i units at quarter load are about 51 dB(A), still quieter on paper than one Predator 3500, and they give 4,400 W.

What generator is quiet enough for a campground?

National Parks set 60 dB(A) at 50 feet, and many campgrounds add quiet hours. Every unit on this page except the Predator 9500 is under that at light load on paper. For overnight CPAP use, a power station is silent and avoids quiet-hour rules altogether.

Did you test these generators?

No. We did not run or measure them. The rankings use manufacturers' published noise figures and specs, and prices read on maker or retailer pages on November 21, 2026. If you own one and have a sound-meter reading with a photo, you can submit it as a verified owner test.
