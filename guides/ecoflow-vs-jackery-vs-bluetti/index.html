EcoFlow vs Jackery vs BLUETTI: which one should you buy?

No brand wins everything, so buy the model, not the logo. EcoFlow is the pick when you want a battery that grows and switches over fast: the DELTA 3 Plus has a published <10 ms UPS, 1,000 W of solar input and expands to 5 kWh. Jackery wins on weight and simplicity: the Explorer 1000 v2 is 23.8 lb for 1,070 Wh, but its v2 models cap solar at 400 W and don't expand. BLUETTI wins on value: the AC180 is the cheapest per watt-hour here ($0.43/Wh) but out of stock until late November, and the AC200L costs the same $899 as a Jackery 2000 v2 while adding 1,200 W solar, 7,200 W surge and expansion to 8 kWh, at 62.4 lb.

What each brand does best, and where it falls short

EcoFlow

Best at: charging speed, solar input per size, expansion and published UPS speed (<10 ms on the DELTA 3 Plus and RIVER 3 Plus, 10 ms on the DELTA Pro 3). The deepest range, from a 286 Wh RIVER to a 48 kWh DELTA Pro 3 system.

Watch for: the highest price per watt-hour of the three on most models, and no published UPS time for the DELTA 2 Max. The DELTA Pro 3's 5-year warranty needs app registration for the last 2 years.

Jackery

Best at: weight and simplicity. The Explorer 1000 v2 (23.8 lb) and 2000 v2 (39.5 lb) are the lightest units here for their capacity, both with <20 ms switchover, and sale prices are aggressive.

Watch for: 400 W solar input on the v2 models, no expansion on the v2 models, a warranty that is 3 + 2 years only through Jackery or authorized sellers, and Jackery's recommendation to use only Jackery panels. Battery cycles are rated to 70%, not 80%.

BLUETTI

Best at: price per watt-hour and big inputs. The AC200L takes 1,200 W of solar on a wide 12–145 V window and 2,400 W of AC, and BLUETTI is the only one of the three that publishes inverter self-consumption, so runtime math is honest.

Watch for: weight, thinner stock (the AC180 and AC70 were out of stock on October 21), Bluetooth-only app on the AC180, and no listed UPS time on the AC180.

Decision 2: how long the outage lasts

Runtime is nameplate Wh × 0.85 ÷ (load W + about 15 W). The 15 W is the inverter's own draw, which BLUETTI publishes for the AC180 (runtime formula) and owners have measured at about 17.5 W on a DELTA 3 Plus (owner test); the other makers don't publish it, so we use 15 W for every model. Expansion is the decision people regret most. A 1 kWh unit that takes an extra battery lets you start cheap and grow into multi-day backup; one that doesn't means buying a second station later. Wi-Fi matters if you want outage alerts or to check charge from work; Bluetooth-only apps work only in the same room. EcoFlow's DELTA 3 Plus app adds time-of-use and storm-warning charging, and the RIVER 3 Plus can tell a PC or NAS to shut down on low battery.

Questions

Which is better, EcoFlow or Jackery?

It depends on what you value. EcoFlow charges faster, takes far more solar (1,000 W on the DELTA 3 Plus vs 400 W on the Jackery Explorer 1000 v2), expands to 5 kWh and publishes a faster UPS switchover (<10 ms vs <20 ms). Jackery is lighter (23.8 lb vs 27.6 lb for about 1 kWh) and cheaper on sale ($519 vs $719 on October 21, 2026). For a grab-and-go 1 kWh box, Jackery; for home backup you may grow, EcoFlow.

Is BLUETTI as good as EcoFlow and Jackery?

On published specs, yes, and it is often the value pick. The AC180 is the cheapest per watt-hour of the models we priced ($0.43/Wh), and the AC200L lists 2,400 W continuous, 7,200 W surge, 1,200 W solar, a 20 ms UPS and expansion to 8,192 Wh for $899, the same price as the lighter Jackery 2000 v2. BLUETTI is also the only one of the three that publishes its inverter self-consumption (about 15 W on the AC180). The trade-offs are weight (the AC200L is 62.4 lb) and that the AC180 lists no UPS switchover time.

Which brand is best for a CPAP?

Pick a model, not a brand, and pick one with a published UPS switchover time so an outage doesn't wake you: the EcoFlow DELTA 3 Plus and RIVER 3 Plus list <10 ms, the Jackery Explorer 1000 v2 and 2000 v2 list <20 ms. Turn off auto-off timers (Jackery's Energy Saving Mode is on by default). Note that Jackery says its stations are not recommended for medical devices and EcoFlow says its products are not for life-sustaining equipment. Ask your doctor, and see our CPAP guide for nights per setup.

Which brand has the best warranty?

All three advertise 5 years on current models, with fine print. Jackery's is 3 years standard plus 2 years extended, and only for units bought from Jackery.com, Jackery-branded third-party stores or authorized dealers; Amazon buyers register the unit on Jackery's site for the extra 2 years; refurbished units get 6 months. EcoFlow lists 5 years on the DELTA 3 Plus, DELTA 2 Max and RIVER 2 Pro, and on the DELTA Pro 3 it is 3 years plus 2 more when you register in the EcoFlow app. BLUETTI lists 5 years on the AC180 and AC200L.

Can I add batteries later?

With EcoFlow and BLUETTI, often yes; with Jackery's v2 models, no. The DELTA 3 Plus takes EcoFlow extra batteries up to 5 kWh, the DELTA 2 Max up to 6,144 Wh, the DELTA Pro 3 up to 12 kWh per unit. BLUETTI's AC200L goes to 8,192 Wh with two B300 packs, but the AC180 can only be charged from a BLUETTI battery, not expanded. Jackery says the Explorer 1000 v2 and 2000 v2 are not expandable, and its page says the Battery Pack 1000 Plus has been discontinued; the Explorer 2000 Plus is Jackery's expandable option.

How long will a 1,000 Wh power station run my essentials?

Use nameplate Wh × 0.85 ÷ (load W + about 15 W of inverter draw). A 100 W load (router, modem, a few LED lights and phone charging) runs about 7.6 hours on a DELTA 3 Plus, 7.9 hours on a Jackery 1000 v2 and 8.5 hours on a BLUETTI AC180. A 2 kWh unit roughly doubles that.
