Best power station for a refrigerator in a power outage

For a full-size fridge, buy a station with a published motor-start surge of at least 1,200 W and about 2 kWh of battery. That keeps a typical bottom-freezer or French-door model running about 23 hours, counting about 15 W of inverter draw. A 1 kWh station covers a top-freezer for about 15–16 hours, which is enough for an overnight outage.

Step 1: know what your fridge actually uses

Running watts on the label tell you almost nothing about runtime, because the compressor cycles on and off. What matters is energy per day. The yellow EnergyGuide label lists kWh per year; divide by 365 for kWh per day. If you don't have the label handy, these are the numbers for 2,636 full-size refrigerators in the ENERGY STAR certified list (pulled October 18, 2026):

Average draw = kWh/yr × 1,000 ÷ 8,760 h. These are DOE test-procedure figures, not outage conditions. A warm kitchen, frequent door openings and a running ice maker all push real use up, so leave headroom. For comparison, the compact Danby DCR032C1WDB is rated 218 kWh/yr and a 9 cu ft Whirlpool chest freezer 201 kWh/yr. The 17.5 cu ft Samsung RT18DG6300 top-freezer is 333 kWh/yr.

Step 2: make sure it can start the compressor

This is where cheap setups fail. A compressor pulls several times its running power for a fraction of a second at start-up. Haier publishes 805 W at start-up vs 195.5 W running (7.0 A vs 1.7 A) for its 18 and 21 cu ft refrigerators (Haier), and Champion's generator chart plans for 150–400 W running and 800–1,200 W starting for a refrigerator (Champion).

So the rule here is simple: the station's maker must publish a surge (peak) figure that clears about 1,200 W and describe it as real surge capacity. "Boost" modes such as EcoFlow X-Boost or BLUETTI Power Lifting lower the voltage to keep resistive loads like kettles running. They are not motor-start ratings, so we don't count them.

In our data these stations have enough running watts but no published motor-start surge, so we can't clear them for a fridge: Anker SOLIX C800, Goal Zero Yeti 1000X, EcoFlow DELTA 2, BLUETTI AC180, Anker SOLIX F2000 (PowerHouse 767). If you already own one, test it with your fridge on a normal day before you rely on it.

Stretch the battery without risking the food

  • Keep the doors shut. FoodSafety.gov says a closed fridge keeps food safe for up to 4 hours with no power, and a full freezer holds about 48 hours (24 if half full).
  • Prioritize the fridge, not the freezer. A full freezer can coast for a day or two on its own, so the fridge gets the battery first.
  • Run it in blocks if you're short. Because a closed fridge holds for up to 4 hours, you can run it a few hours, unplug, and repeat. Watch a fridge thermometer: perishables above 40°F for more than 2 hours go on FoodSafety.gov's discard list.
  • Turn off what you don't need. Ice maker off, outputs you aren't using off, and the fridge on its own outlet.
  • Test it before the storm. Plug the fridge into the station for a full day on a normal day and note the percentage used. That number beats any table, including ours.

Apartment, house, RV: what changes

Apartment or condo

You probably can't run a generator: the CDC says never indoors, in a garage, or within 20 feet of windows, doors and vents, and a balcony rarely clears that. A power station is the safe option indoors. Weight matters if it lives in a closet: the Explorer 1000 v2 is 23.8 lb and the Explorer 2000 v2 39.5 lb. The DELTA 2 Max is 50 lb.

House with a fridge and a freezer

A 9 cu ft chest freezer adds about 550 Wh a day (roughly 650 Wh of station capacity), and plan for both compressors starting at once. That is where a high-surge unit like the BLUETTI AC200L (7,200 W surge) or the SOLIX F3800 (9,000 W) earns its weight. If you also want the sump pump and furnace fan, look at a generator with a 30 amp inlet instead.

RV or van

RV fridges are a different animal. Absorption units (propane or 120 V) and 12 V compressor fridges aren't in the ENERGY STAR household list, so read the label or manual for watts or amps. As a planning range, Champion's chart lists an RV refrigerator at 150–600 W running and 400–1,000 W starting. A 12 V compressor fridge can run straight off the station's 12 V car port and skip inverter losses. The Jackery 1000 v2 and 2000 v2 car ports are rated 12 V⎓10 A. See best power station for an RV.

Alternatives worth considering

  • Inverter generator. Better for long outages because you refuel instead of recharge, and a Honda EU2200i-class unit starts a fridge easily. Outdoors only (CDC). Compare in generator vs power station.
  • Power station plus generator. Run the generator a few hours in the daytime to refill the station, then run the fridge quietly overnight.
  • Coolers and ice. For a short outage, moving the essentials to a cooler costs less than any battery.
  • Whole-home backup. If outages here last days, a transfer switch or inlet plus a larger generator covers the fridge, furnace and well pump together. See best generator for home backup.

Questions

Can a 1,000 W power station run a full-size refrigerator?

Usually, if its surge is rated for motors. A full-size fridge typically runs at 150–400 W but needs 800–1,200 W for a moment when the compressor starts (Champion's wattage chart; Haier lists 805 W at start-up for its 18 and 21 cu ft models). Pick a station whose maker publishes a surge figure above about 1,200 W, not just a resistive-load boost mode.

How long will a power station run a refrigerator?

Usable watt-hours divided by the fridge's average draw plus about 15 W of inverter draw. Using the median ENERGY STAR figures, a 1,070 Wh Jackery Explorer 1000 v2 runs a top-freezer about 16 hours and a bottom-freezer about 12 hours. A 2,048 Wh EcoFlow DELTA 2 Max runs them about 31 and 23 hours.

What size power station do I need for a fridge for 3 days?

About 4.8 kWh of nameplate capacity for a median top-freezer, and 6.5 kWh for a median bottom-freezer or French door, counting about 15 W of inverter draw. That is past most single portable stations, so plan on an expansion battery, solar or generator recharge, or running the fridge in blocks.

Is it safe to unplug the fridge for part of the outage?

FoodSafety.gov says a closed refrigerator keeps food safe for up to 4 hours without power, and a full freezer holds about 48 hours (24 if half full). Perishables held at 40°F or above for more than 2 hours go on its discard list. A thermometer tells you where you stand.

Can I run a fridge on a generator instead?

Yes, a 2,000 W class inverter generator handles a fridge start, but the CDC says to run it outdoors only, more than 20 feet from windows, doors and vents, with a battery CO alarm inside. Apartments and condos usually can't do that, which is why a power station is the default there.
