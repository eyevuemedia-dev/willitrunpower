Best power station for an RV: what it can run, and what it can't

A power station is a great RV battery for lights, fans, the water pump, the furnace blower, a CPAP and laptops. It is a poor substitute for a generator if you want the roof air conditioner all night. Micro-Air puts a common 13,500 BTU roof unit at about 15 A, or 1,800 W, on a hot day. Even the 4,096 Wh EcoFlow DELTA Pro 3 runs that for about 1.9 hours with the compressor on nonstop, and a full 8-hour night needs about 17,082 Wh. For a 30 A trailer, the stations that make life easy have a built-in TT-30 outlet: the BLUETTI AC200L, the Anker SOLIX F2000 (PowerHouse 767) and the EcoFlow DELTA Pro 3. The Anker SOLIX F3800 adds a 14-50 for 50 A rigs.

What RV appliances actually draw

RVs run on two systems: 120 V AC from shore power, a generator or an inverter, and 12 V DC from the house battery. Most of what runs overnight is on the 12 V side. These are maker figures for common RV equipment:

The roof air conditioner: running watts, starting watts and soft starters

Running is the easy part. Micro-Air, which makes the EasyStart soft starter, says the two most common RV units, 13,500 and 15,000 BTU, draw about 15 A and 16.2 A on a hot summer day: 1,800 W and 1,944 W at 120 V. Every station in the table from 2,400 W continuous up can carry that running load. Micro-Air also notes that generators lose about 3.5% of their output per 1,000 feet of elevation; a battery station does not have that problem.

Starting is the hard part. Dometic's Penguin II spec sheet lists the 13,500 BTU compressor at 63 A locked-rotor and the fan at 8.5 A, about 8,580 W on paper, and asks for at least a 3.5 kW generator for one unit (5.0 kW for two). The smaller 11,000 BTU model lists 53 A locked-rotor and a 2.5 kW minimum. Compare that with the surge figures above: only the F3800's 9,000 W is above 8,580 W. Real compressor starts usually peak below locked-rotor amps and last a fraction of a second, so some stations do start roof units, but no station maker publishes a test with a named roof AC. Willrun does not clear a compressor start without one.

The practical fix is a soft starter. It ramps the compressor up instead of slamming it on. Micro-Air says a 13.5K unit with an EasyStart can start on a 2,000 W inverter generator. Installing one means opening the AC's electrical box, so follow the maker's instructions or have an RV tech do it.

What a night of AC costs. Eight hours with the compressor running nonstop is about 17,082 Wh from the station. Half the time is still about 8,612 Wh. Nonstop, that is the stored energy of four DELTA Pro 3s for one hot night (two at half duty), which is why most RVers who want overnight AC run a generator, use shore power, or run the AC only in the evening and switch to fans.

Plugging the shore cord into a station

The simplest RV hookup is the one you already use at a campground: plug the shore cord into the station. A TT-30R outlet takes a standard 30 A RV plug directly. Three things to know first:

  1. A TT-30 outlet is not a 30 A service. A campground pedestal gives 30 A × 120 V = 3,600 W. The BLUETTI AC200L's TT-30 shares its 2,400 W total with the other outlets, and the Anker F2000 lists 2,400 W. Run the AC or the microwave, not both.
  2. The converter will drain the station. As soon as the RV sees 120 V, the converter starts charging the house batteries. A Progressive Dynamics PD4655 can pull up to 950 W while it does. On a 1,070 Wh station that is about 0.9 hours. Flip the converter breaker off unless you want to move energy into the house battery (and accept the losses: the converter is over 80% efficient, so some energy is lost on top of the inverter's share).
  3. Everything on 120 V wakes up. An absorption fridge set to AUTO switches to its 325 W electric element, and an electric water-heater element can switch on too. Set the fridge and water heater to propane before you connect.

The alternative is to skip the shore cord and plug a few things straight into the station: a CPAP, a laptop, a fan, a coffee maker. That avoids the converter loop entirely.

Now the losses. If the station feeds the shore cord all day, the 12 V loads go through the inverter and the converter, and the inverter's own 15 W runs for 24 hours. That turns 743 Wh at the devices into about 1,500 Wh from the station. Running the 12 V loads from the house battery and using the station only for AC loads like the CPAP is far more efficient; recharge the house battery from solar, the tow vehicle or a short converter session.

Rule of thumb from this example: a 1 kWh station handles a night of 12 V loads and a CPAP if you skip the converter loop. A 2 kWh station covers a cool weekend through the shore cord with some solar. Add a roof AC and you are into 4 kWh and up for a few hours, plus a generator or solar for anything longer.

Recharging on the road

  • Solar. Portable panels or a roof array feeding the station's solar input. Output depends on season and location; our sister site SolarSized has location-based sizing.
  • Generator. An inverter generator recharges the station faster than solar and can carry the AC directly. Run it outdoors, away from doors, windows and vents, and never inside the RV or an enclosed space.
  • Tow vehicle or campground. Car-port charging is slow (typically 100–120 W). At a powered site, top up the station from the pedestal before heading off-grid.

Questions

Can a power station run an RV air conditioner?

Yes, for a few hours, not all night. Micro-Air puts a 13,500 BTU roof unit at about 15 A (1,800 W) and a 15,000 BTU unit at about 16.2 A (1,944 W) on a hot day. With our formula (Wh × 0.85 ÷ (load + 15 W)) a 2,048 Wh station runs a 13.5K unit about 1.0 hours and the 4,096 Wh EcoFlow DELTA Pro 3 about 1.9 hours if the compressor never cycles off. Starting the compressor is a separate problem; see the next answer.

Do I need a soft starter to run an RV AC on a power station?

Plan on one. Dometic's spec sheet lists 63 A locked-rotor amps for the Penguin II 13,500 BTU compressor plus 8.5 A for the fan, about 8,580 W at 120 V on paper. That is above every station's published surge figure in our table except the Anker SOLIX F3800's 9,000 W. Real starts usually peak below locked-rotor amps, but no station maker publishes a test with a specific roof unit. A soft starter such as the Micro-Air EasyStart lowers the start current; Micro-Air says a 13.5K unit with EasyStart can start on a 2,000 W inverter generator.

Can I plug my RV shore cord into a power station?

Yes, if the station has a TT-30 outlet (or with a 30 A to 15/20 A adapter on a regular outlet, which limits you to that outlet's rating). Two warnings. A TT-30 outlet on a 2,400 W station is not a 30 A service: 30 A at 120 V is 3,600 W. And the RV's converter will start charging the house batteries from the station. A Progressive Dynamics PD4655 converter can draw up to 950 W, which would empty a 1,070 Wh station in about 0.9 hours. Turn the converter breaker off unless you mean to charge the house batteries.

What size power station do I need for an RV?

Size it from your loads. Our example boondocking day (furnace blower 4 hours, pump, roof fan, 40 W of LED lights, a CPAP) adds up to about 743 Wh at the device, but about 1,500 Wh from the station if everything goes through the shore cord, the converter (over 80% efficient per Progressive Dynamics) and an inverter left on all day. That points to a 2 kWh station for a weekend with solar, or a 1 kWh station only if you run 12 V loads from the house battery. Roof AC moves you to 4 kWh and up, or a generator.

Can a power station run an RV fridge?

A compressor fridge, yes. A typical absorption fridge on electric, poorly. Dometic's manual for the RM2652/RM2852 lists a 325 W, 2.7 A heating element on 120 V. Even if it heated half the time, that is about 5,012 Wh a day with inverter draw. Run an absorption fridge on propane, where it only needs 12 V for its controls; note Dometic says these fridges cannot cool on 12 V alone.

Will a power station run my RV furnace?

Yes, it is one of the best uses. The furnace burns propane; electricity only runs the blower and controls. Suburban's service manual lists 6.5 A at 12 V for SF-20/25/30 models (about 82 W), 9.0 A for the SF-35 and 11.5 A for the SF-42. Four hours of blower time on an SF-30 is about 328 Wh at the battery.

Is a power station better than a generator for an RV?

For quiet hours, short stops and small loads, yes: no fuel, no noise, no exhaust. For running a roof AC through a hot afternoon or several days off-grid without sun, a generator or a big solar array still wins. Many RVers use both: a station for overnight and quiet hours, and a generator or solar to recharge it. If you run a generator, keep it outdoors and away from windows, doors and vents.
