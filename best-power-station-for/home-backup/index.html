Best power station for home backup

On stored energy alone, the EcoFlow DELTA Pro 3 (4,096 Wh) outlasts the Anker SOLIX F3800 (3,840 Wh), while the F3800 has the higher continuous output at 6,000 W. A 1,500 W Lasko heater needs at least 1,500 W continuous or a documented resistive boost.

Home backup in this set is a fridge, a CPAP, a router, a light, and maybe a heater or a sump pump. It is not a central air conditioner. The 24-hour and 3-day planners add those loads up.

You should get…

  • the EcoFlow DELTA Pro 3 if you want the most stored energy in one box: 4,096 Wh, about 30 h at a steady 100 W, 120/240 V. It was $2,799 and sold out at EcoFlow on Nov 19.
  • the Anker SOLIX F3800 (now sold as the F3800 Plus) if output matters most: 6,000 W continuous and 240 V. $2,999.99, in stock at Anker on Nov 19. Its roughly 40 W idle cuts its hours at small loads.
  • the EcoFlow DELTA 2 Max if a fridge, CPAP, router and lights are the whole plan: 2,048 Wh, about 15 h at 100 W, expandable to 6,144 Wh. $1,029, in stock on Nov 19.
  • the Jackery Explorer 2000 v2 if it has to be carried: the same 2 kWh class at 39.5 lb, but no expansion battery and 400 W solar. $999, in stock on Nov 19.

How we ranked, heaviest criterion first

  1. Stored energy: hours at a steady 100 W, counting about 15 W of inverter idle (about 40 W for the F3800, per Anker).
  2. Continuous output, so a 1,500 W heater, a sump pump or 240 V loads fit.
  3. A published motor-start surge for fridge and pump starts.
  4. Growth path: expansion batteries and solar input.
  5. Weight, then maker price and stock on November 19, 2026.

We did not test these stations ourselves. Rankings use the makers' published specs, our runtime math (nameplate Wh × 0.85 ÷ (load + inverter idle)) and the prices and stock we read on the date shown. Affiliate links never change a ranking.

Best power station for home backup?

On stored energy alone, the EcoFlow DELTA Pro 3 (4,096 Wh) outlasts the Anker SOLIX F3800 (3,840 Wh), while the F3800 has the higher continuous output at 6,000 W. A 1,500 W Lasko heater needs at least 1,500 W continuous or a documented resistive boost.

What does the hours column measure?

Hours at a steady 100 W draw. The hours use nameplate watt-hours times 0.85, divided by the average draw named in that column. They are not a lab test of the station.

Why is the list short?

Only stations with a published spec in this set are ranked, and only the first rows are shown. A model that cannot hold the running watts is omitted from the fridge, CPAP, and RV lists. The sump-pump list keeps the stations that will not run it so the cutoff is visible.
