Best power station for a CPAP

Medical safety notice: Will It Run? gives general power estimates only, not medical advice. Power draw varies by model, settings, pressure, humidifier and altitude. Before relying on any battery, power station, inverter or generator for a medical device, confirm approved power sources with the device manufacturer and your doctor or equipment supplier, and test your setup before an emergency. Never change prescribed settings to extend battery life. If your therapy is life-sustaining, keep the backup your supplier provides, register with your utility's medical-needs program, and have an evacuation plan. Run generators outdoors only, at least 20 ft from windows and doors, because of carbon monoxide risk. In an emergency, call 911.

For bedside outage backup, get a 1 kWh LiFePO4 station with a published UPS switchover time, such as the EcoFlow DELTA 3 Plus (<10 ms). On AC that is about 3.2 nights of an AirSense 11 with the humidifier, or 1.6 with a heated tube. For camping with the humidifier off, run the ResMed DC converter from a 12 V port: a 768 Wh RIVER 2 Pro then lasts up to about 12 nights.

This guide works through the four decisions that actually change what you should buy: whether you use the humidifier and heated tube, AC or DC, how many nights you need to cover, and whether it is for outages at home or for camping. Every watt figure comes from ResMed, Philips or the station maker and is linked in Sources. Recommendations are based on that power math, not on which brand pays more.

Decision 1: humidifier, heated tube, or neither

The humidifier and heated tube decide the size of station, not the CPAP itself. Here are ResMed's measured figures for the AirSense 11 AutoSet, from its therapy-device power table:

AirSense 11 settingAverage drawPer 8 h night (CPAP only)Station capacity per night on AC*
No humidifier7 W56 Wh207 Wh
HumidAir 11 humidifier19 W152 Wh320 Wh
Humidifier + ClimateLineAir 11 heated tube55 W440 Wh659 Wh

*Nameplate Wh, including about 15 W of inverter draw and 15% conversion loss. ResMed notes its values are lab measurements and a home meter can read differently. The AirSense 11 user guide lists 56.1 W typical and 73.2 W peak for the whole system on its 65 W power supply.

Two things stand out. The heated tube nearly triples the humidifier's draw (19 W to 55 W), so the tube, not the tub, is what drains a battery. And with no humidifier, a CPAP uses so little that the station's own electronics become the biggest load. ResMed's AirSense 10 user guide says to turn the humidifier off when running from an external battery to get the most runtime. If humidity was prescribed for dryness or nosebleeds, ask your doctor before you drop it. Buy a bigger station rather than changing therapy on your own.

Other machines: AirSense 10 is 7 W / 22 W / 34 W for the same three settings, and the AirCurve 11 VAuto BiPAP is 8 W / 34 W / 66 W (see BiPAP battery backup). Philips doesn't publish a typical DreamStation draw, so we plan for its 80 W power supply rating as a worst case (DreamStation page). The full list is on the CPAP watts page.

Decision 2: DC cord or the AC outlet

Why it matters: an inverter uses power just to stay on. BLUETTI's AC180 runtime formula counts about 15 W of self-consumption. Owner tests posted to r/Ecoflow_community measured the DELTA 3 Plus at about 17.5 W idle on recent firmware. Against a 7 W CPAP, that idle draw is more than the CPAP. That is why simple "capacity ÷ watts" calculators overstate humidifier-off nights on AC by up to three times. Every CPAP and BiPAP table on this site now counts it (updated October 20, 2026).

What the DC converters need:

  • ResMed Air11 DC/DC converter (AirSense 11): 12 V or 24 V in at 10 A / 5 A, 24 V / 3.75 A (90 W) out. ResMed says it can run the AirSense 11 with the HumidAir humidifier and ClimateLineAir heated tube (ResMed). Most station car ports are rated 12 V at 10 A, which matches.
  • ResMed Air10 DC/DC converter 90W (AirSense 10, AirCurve 10): 12/24 V in at 13 A / 6.5 A, 51 W typical and 106 W peak. ResMed notes the humidifier "uses considerably more power" (user guide). A 13 A input rating is above a 10 A car port, so expect the heated humidifier to trip some ports. On DC, the humidifier-off setting is the safe bet.
  • Philips: use only the Philips Respironics DC cord, and the DreamStation 2 battery accessory does not support humidification (DreamStation 2 manual). More detail is in CPAP on DC vs AC.

Test before you rely on it. Car ports have overload protection. One owner review on Jackery's Explorer 1000 v2 page reports the car port cutting out with an AirSense 11 DC cable and switching to AC instead. Run a full night at home first.

How that maps to a purchase:

  • Overnight outages (1 night): 300–500 Wh covers you with the humidifier on. Over 700 Wh if you keep the heated tube.

Outage at home: what to check before the storm

  • UPS / pass-through mode. Keep the station on the wall charger with the CPAP plugged into it, and an outage becomes a non-event. Jackery calls its 20 ms switch a "non-professional UPS function" and says to test it several times before relying on it (Explorer 1000 v2 manual). Do the test: with the CPAP running, pull the station's wall plug and see whether the machine keeps blowing.
  • Turn off auto-shutoff timers. Jackery's Energy Saving Mode is on by default and turns all outputs off after 12 hours when the AC load is 25 W or less, which describes a CPAP (manual). EcoFlow's safety instructions say to set the AC timeout to "never" in the app for critical loads (EcoFlow). Check the same setting on any brand.
  • Noise and light. The DELTA 3 Plus, RIVER 3 Plus and both Jackerys claim about 30 dB at light load. Fans spin up while the station is recharging, so recharge in another room if you can. Dim or cover the screen.
  • Recharge plan. Solar: see what size panel a CPAP needs. Car: most stations take 12 V car charging, but slowly (Jackery lists 12 hours for the 1000 v2). Generator: run it in the daytime, outdoors only (CDC), and refill the station.
  • Keep it topped up. A station sitting at 40% in the closet does you no good at 2 a.m. Anker, for example, says to check a stored C1000 weekly and recharge it to 100% if it drops below 30% (Anker). Or just leave it on the wall charger in UPS mode.

Camping, RV and travel

  • Weight is the constraint. For a tent, the RIVER 3 Plus (10.4 lb) or RIVER 2 Pro (18.2 lb) are carryable. Anything at 1 kWh is 24–28 lb, and the 2 kWh units are 40–50 lb, which is car-camping or RV territory.
  • Use DC if you can. With the humidifier off and the ResMed DC converter, a 768 Wh station is good for a week-long trip on paper (12 nights best case). The RIVER 3 Plus has no 12 V car port, so it can only run a CPAP on AC.
  • Cold nights. Most stations won't charge below freezing (the Jackerys, RIVER 3 Plus and AC180 all list 32°F as the minimum charging temperature). Keep the station in the tent or vehicle overnight. ResMed lists 41°F to 95°F as the operating range for the AirSense 11 and its DC converter.
  • Flying: the FAA limits spare lithium-ion batteries to 100 Wh each in carry-on bags, or up to two at 101–160 Wh with airline approval (FAA PackSafe). No power station qualifies. Fly with a CPAP-specific battery under 100 Wh, and check your airline's rules.

What the station makers say about medical use

Read this before you buy. Jackery's product FAQ says its power stations "are not recommended for use with medical devices, life-support equipment" or other uses where an interruption could cause harm. EcoFlow's safety instructions say its products are not intended for life-sustaining equipment such as hospital-grade CPAP or ventilators, and to check with the equipment maker before powering other medical devices. Its products terms of use also tell users to watch the battery level so it doesn't run out. A home CPAP on a power station is common and usually works. Still, none of these companies guarantee it, so treat the station as a backup you have tested, not as medical equipment.

Alternatives

  • A CPAP-specific battery. Smaller and airline-friendly, but usually only a night or two with the humidifier off. Ask your supplier which ones are approved for your machine.
  • A car or deep-cycle battery with the DC cord. ResMed recommends a deep-cycle battery for its converter. The math is in can a car battery run a CPAP?
  • An inverter generator. Unlimited runtime if you have fuel, but it has to run outdoors, away from windows. A good pairing: a generator recharges the station in the daytime, and the station runs the CPAP silently overnight. Compare in generator vs power station.
  • Somewhere with power. For a long outage, a relative's house, a hotel or a shelter beats rationing therapy.

Questions

What size power station do I need for a CPAP?

Work from your setting, not the machine. ResMed measures an AirSense 11 at 7 W with no humidifier, 19 W with the humidifier and 55 W with the humidifier plus a ClimateLineAir heated tube. On AC, add the station's own inverter draw (BLUETTI publishes about 15 W for its AC180). That is about 320 Wh of nameplate capacity per night with the humidifier, and 659 Wh with a heated tube. Multiply by the nights you want to cover.

Can I use a power station as a UPS for my CPAP?

Many can. Leave the station plugged into the wall and the CPAP plugged into the station. If the grid drops, it switches to battery. Published switchover times include under 10 ms for the EcoFlow DELTA 3 Plus and RIVER 3 Plus, and under 20 ms for the Jackery Explorer 1000 v2 and 2000 v2 and the Anker SOLIX C1000. Jackery calls this a non-professional UPS function and says to test it several times, so pull the wall plug while your CPAP is running and check that it keeps going.

Is DC or AC better for running a CPAP from a power station?

With the humidifier off, DC is much better. A station's inverter uses power just to stay on, about 15 W on the BLUETTI AC180 by the maker's own figure, which is twice what an AirSense 11 uses with no humidifier. The ResMed DC converter plugs into the 12 V car port and skips the inverter. With a heated humidifier the difference matters less, and some car ports may not handle the converter's peak current.

How many nights will a 1,000 Wh power station run a CPAP?

For an AirSense 11 on AC, counting about 15 W of inverter draw: about 4.9 nights with the humidifier off, 3.2 with the humidifier and 1.6 with a heated tube, on a 1,024 Wh station. On the DC converter with the humidifier off, the upper bound is about 16 nights.

Can I take a power station on a plane for my CPAP?

No. The FAA limits spare lithium-ion batteries to 100 Wh each in carry-on bags, or up to two batteries of 101 to 160 Wh with airline approval. The smallest station here is 286 Wh. For flights, use a CPAP battery rated 100 Wh or less and check with your airline.

Do power station makers say it is OK to run a CPAP?

They hedge. Jackery's product FAQ says its stations are not recommended for medical devices. EcoFlow's safety instructions say its products are not intended for life-sustaining equipment such as hospital-grade CPAP, and to check with the equipment maker before powering other medical devices. Treat a power station as a convenience backup, and follow your doctor's and supplier's advice.
