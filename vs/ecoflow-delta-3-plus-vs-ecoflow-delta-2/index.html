EcoFlow DELTA 3 Plus vs DELTA 2: UPS, surge, solar and the DELTA 3

Same battery, same 1,800 W inverter, almost the same weight. The DELTA 3 Plus wins on the things that matter in an outage: a 10 ms UPS switchover instead of 30 ms, 3,600 W surge instead of 2,700 W, 1,500 W wall charging, 1,000 W of solar input and expansion to 5 kWh. It was $719 and in stock at EcoFlow US on November 17, 2026. EcoFlow's US store now redirects the DELTA 2 product link to the DELTA 3 ($579), which has the same UPS, surge and charging as the Plus but one 500 W solar input and slower USB. Buy the DELTA 3 Plus for solar or fast USB-C; the DELTA 3 if you don't need those; a DELTA 2 only when it costs clearly less than a DELTA 3.

Short answer

  • Runtime is a tie. All three hold 1,024 Wh of LiFePO4, 870 Wh usable in our math. No model runs a load meaningfully longer.
  • UPS: DELTA 3 Plus and DELTA 3 list under 10 ms. EcoFlow's DELTA 2 manual says within 30 ms and calls it a basic UPS that does not support 0 ms switching. For a desktop PC or a bedside CPAP on AC, 10 ms is the safer bet; test either way.
  • Motor starts: 3,600 W surge on the DELTA 3 series vs 2,700 W on the DELTA 2 (US manual, 120 V). Both still need your appliance's published startup watts to be sure.
  • Charging: 1,500 W from the wall (EcoFlow: 0–100% in 56 min on the Plus) vs 1,200 W (80 min) on the DELTA 2. Solar: 2 × 500 W on the Plus, 500 W on the DELTA 3 and DELTA 2.
  • Expansion: DELTA 3 series up to 5 kWh, and it accepts the DELTA 2 Extra Battery via EcoFlow's XT150 cable. DELTA 2 takes one extra battery (EcoFlow: 1–3 kWh).
  • Our pick: DELTA 3 Plus at $719 if you will use solar or charge laptops at 140 W; DELTA 3 at $579 for plain outage backup. Keep a DELTA 2 you already own; buy one only at a clear discount to $579.

Is the DELTA 2 discontinued?

EcoFlow has not published a discontinuation notice that we could find, but on November 17, 2026 its US store sent the DELTA 2 product link straight to the DELTA 3 page, and the DELTA 2 is no longer offered as a variant there. EcoFlow's informational DELTA 2 page is still live. In practice, new DELTA 2 units now come from third-party sellers and clearance stock, and used ones from marketplaces.

That changes the real question. Against the DELTA 2, the DELTA 3 is the like-for-like replacement: same capacity and output, faster UPS, more surge, faster wall charging, 1,000 more rated cycles, and the same single 500 W solar input. The DELTA 3 Plus adds a second solar input, 140 W USB-C and faster USB-A for $140 more at today's EcoFlow prices.

If you own a DELTA 2: there is no runtime reason to replace it. Upgrade only if you need the 10 ms UPS, more surge, or solar beyond 500 W. If you have a DELTA 2 Extra Battery, EcoFlow lists it as compatible with the DELTA 3 series through the Alternator Charger XT150 output cable, so it carries over.

UPS switchover: 10 ms vs 30 ms

Both stations pass grid power through to their AC outlets while plugged in and switch to battery when the grid drops. EcoFlow lists under 10 ms for the DELTA 3 series. The DELTA 2 manual says it switches within 30 ms, calls this a basic UPS function that does not support 0 ms switching, and tells owners not to use it for data servers or workstations that need 0 ms, and to test compatibility first (DELTA 2 manual).

  • Desktop PCs and NAS boxes: some PCs ride through a 30 ms gap and some reboot; it depends on the power supply. The 10 ms models are the better fit; EcoFlow also advertises NAS support (USB HID via its PowerManager app) for the DELTA 3 Plus.
  • Bedside CPAP: CPAPs have their own power supply and many tolerate brief gaps, but no maker we know publishes a ride-through time. Whichever station you choose, unplug it from the wall with the CPAP running and confirm therapy continues.
  • Routers, lights, fridges: either one works; a 30 ms blip is not noticeable to these loads.

Neither is an online double-conversion UPS. If a device must never see a gap, buy a dedicated online UPS.

Surge and motor loads: 3,600 W vs 2,700 W

Both run the same 1,800 W continuous. The difference is how hard they can push for the second or two a compressor or pump motor needs to start. EcoFlow lists 3,600 W surge for the DELTA 3 series and the DELTA 2 manual lists 2,700 W at 120 V. (We corrected our DELTA 2 entry today: it earlier treated that 2,700 W as a 230 V figure.)

  • Where it matters: a refrigerator or chest freezer, a sump pump, a window air conditioner or a well pump at the edge of the rating. A motor whose startup draw falls between 2,700 W and 3,600 W is within the DELTA 3 series' surge rating but over the DELTA 2's.
  • Where it doesn't: we only clear a motor start when the appliance maker publishes startup watts. Most fridge and sump-pump makers don't, so our verdicts for both stations stay "runs, surge risk" for those loads. See the DELTA 2 + Zoeller M53 and DELTA 2 + Danby compact fridge pages.
  • X-Boost is not surge. All three list 2,200 W X-Boost, which lowers voltage so heaters and kettles run without tripping. It does not help motors or electronics.

Runtimes: identical, so plan around idle draw

With the same 1,024 Wh, runtimes are the same on paper. We use 870 Wh usable and add about 15 W for the inverter's own draw on AC (BLUETTI's published self-consumption for its AC180). An owner test measured about 17.5 W idle on a DELTA 3 Plus; EcoFlow does not publish an idle figure for any of the three.

The idle term is why small loads look so short on AC. At 7 W, a CPAP is less than half of what the inverter spends just staying on. Turn the AC output off when you aren't using it, and use DC outputs where you can.

Charging and solar

  • Wall: 1,500 W on the DELTA 3 series vs 1,200 W on the DELTA 2. EcoFlow claims 0–100% in 56 minutes for the Plus and 80 minutes for the DELTA 2. Before a forecast storm, either refills in roughly 1 to 1.5 hours.
  • Solar: the Plus has two 500 W inputs (11–60 V each). Two 400 W panels on separate inputs can deliver up to about 800 W in full sun; EcoFlow's best case is a full charge in 70 minutes at 1,000 W. The DELTA 3 and DELTA 2 top out at 500 W, which in practice means most of a sunny day. For multi-day outages on solar, the Plus's second input is the single biggest functional difference. Panel sizing help: SolarSized.
  • Car: both have a car-charging input, but at about 100 W from a 12 V socket a 1 kWh battery needs 10+ hours. EcoFlow's 800 W Alternator Charger (a separate, installed accessory) cuts that to 1.3 hours on the DELTA 3 series.
  • Generator: any clean inverter generator can charge all three through the AC input. EcoFlow sells a Smart Generator that auto-starts with the DELTA 3 series.

Expansion

The DELTA 3 series reaches 5 kWh with extra batteries. EcoFlow lists the DELTA 3, DELTA 2, DELTA 2 Max and DELTA Pro 3 extra batteries as compatible through the Alternator Charger XT150 output cable. The DELTA 2 connects one Smart Extra Battery; EcoFlow describes it as 1–3 kWh expandable (about 2 kWh total with the 1,024 Wh DELTA 2 Extra Battery, about 3 kWh with the larger DELTA Max Extra Battery). At EcoFlow's current bundle price, the DELTA 3 Plus with a DELTA 3 Series Extra Battery was listed at $1,119 but shown as sold out on November 17.

If you are likely to want 2–3 kWh later, either works. If you might want more, or you want to keep adding solar, the DELTA 3 Plus is the platform to buy. For a full 24-hour or 3-day outage plan, see 24-hour outage and 3-day outage.

CPAP and medical devices

All three have the same 12.6 V 10 A car outlet, so all three work with ResMed's DC/DC converters. On 1,024 Wh, a humidifier-off AirSense 11 (about 7 W, ResMed) gets about 15.5 nights on the DC converter vs about 4.9 on the AC outlet at a 15 W idle allowance; with a heated tube (55 W) it is about 2.0 vs 1.6 nights. Details for each converter and when AC is still the better choice: CPAP on DC vs AC.

For bedside use during outages, the DELTA 3 series' 10 ms switchover is a real advantage over the DELTA 2's 30 ms. The DELTA 2 manual also says its AC output switches off after 12 hours with no load, which won't affect a CPAP plugged into AC; it only matters if you leave AC on with nothing connected. Medical-device picks: best power station for a CPAP and CPAP during a power outage.

Medical safety notice: Will It Run? gives general power estimates only, not medical advice. Power draw varies by model, settings, pressure, humidifier and altitude. Before relying on any battery, power station, inverter or generator for a medical device, confirm approved power sources with the device manufacturer and your doctor or equipment supplier, and test your setup before an emergency. Never change prescribed settings to extend battery life. If your therapy is life-sustaining, keep the backup your supplier provides, register with your utility's medical-needs program, and have an evacuation plan. Run generators outdoors only, at least 20 ft from windows and doors, because of carbon monoxide risk. In an emergency, call 911.

Which to buy

  • Buy the DELTA 3 Plus ($719 at EcoFlow on Nov 17) if you will charge from solar, run a laptop at 140 W, or want the most expandable platform. The second 500 W solar input is worth the $140 over the DELTA 3 for anyone planning multi-day outages.
  • Buy the DELTA 3 ($579) for plain home backup: you get the same 10 ms UPS, 3,600 W surge and 1,500 W wall charging as the Plus.
  • Buy a DELTA 2 only if it is clearly cheaper than $579 new, or a well-priced used unit with a known history, and 30 ms switchover is fine for your devices.
  • Already own a DELTA 2? Keep it. Same runtime; add a DELTA 2 Extra Battery if you need more, and know that pack also works with a future DELTA 3 series unit.

Questions

What is the difference between the EcoFlow DELTA 3 Plus and DELTA 2?

Same 1,024 Wh LiFePO4 battery and 1,800 W output. The DELTA 3 Plus switches to battery in under 10 ms (DELTA 2: within 30 ms), surges to 3,600 W (DELTA 2: 2,700 W), charges at 1,500 W from the wall (1,200 W), takes 1,000 W of solar (500 W), expands to 5 kWh (about 3 kWh) and is rated for 4,000 cycles to 80% (3,000).

Is the EcoFlow DELTA 2 discontinued?

EcoFlow has not published a notice we could find, but on November 17, 2026 its US store redirected the DELTA 2 product link to the DELTA 3. New DELTA 2 units now come mainly from third-party sellers and clearance stock.

DELTA 3 or DELTA 3 Plus?

The DELTA 3 ($579 at EcoFlow US on November 17, 2026) has the same capacity, output, 10 ms UPS, surge and 1,500 W wall charging. The Plus ($719) adds a second 500 W solar input (1,000 W total), 140 W USB-C instead of 100 W and faster USB-A. Pay the difference if you use solar.

Which runs longer?

Neither. All three hold 1,024 Wh, about 870 Wh usable in our math. On AC, inverter idle draw matters more than the model: an owner measured about 17.5 W on a DELTA 3 Plus, and EcoFlow does not publish idle figures.

Does a DELTA 2 extra battery work with the DELTA 3 Plus?

EcoFlow lists the DELTA 2 Extra Battery, along with the DELTA 3, DELTA 2 Max and DELTA Pro 3 extra batteries, as compatible with the DELTA 3 series through the Alternator Charger XT150 output cable, up to 5 kWh.

Which is better for a CPAP?

Runtime is the same: about 15.5 nights for a humidifier-off AirSense 11 on the ResMed DC converter, 4.9 on AC with a 15 W idle allowance. For bedside outage use on AC, the DELTA 3 Plus's under-10 ms switchover beats the DELTA 2's 30 ms. Test with your own machine and confirm power sources with the device maker.
