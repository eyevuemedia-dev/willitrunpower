EcoFlow DELTA 2 Max vs Jackery Explorer 2000 Plus: output, idle draw, expansion and the new v2

The EcoFlow DELTA 2 Max (2,048 Wh, 2,400 W) is the lighter, cheaper 2 kWh box: $1,029 and in stock at EcoFlow on November 15, 2026. The Jackery Explorer 2000 Plus (2,042.8 Wh) adds 600 W more output (3,000 W, with a 30 A TT-30R outlet), expands to about 12 kWh, and can pair two units for 120/240 V, but it weighs 61.5 lb, idles at 25 W, and Jackery's US store now redirects it to the new HomePower 2000 Plus v2 (2,400 W, 41.4 lb, 10 ms switchover, $1,199 and sold out on pre-order). Buy the DELTA 2 Max if 2,400 W covers your list. Buy an Explorer 2000 Plus only if you need the 3,000 W, the TT-30R or a 240 V pair, and can find one at a fair price.

Short answer

  • Same battery, different inverter: both hold about 2,045 Wh of LiFePO4 (1,741 vs 1,736 Wh usable in our math). Capacity is a tie; output, idle draw and expansion decide it.
  • Output: Explorer 2000 Plus 3,000 W continuous / 6,000 W surge, four 20 A outlets plus a 30 A TT-30R. DELTA 2 Max 2,400 W / 4,800 W surge across six outlets. EcoFlow's 3,400 W X-Boost only helps heaters and kettles that tolerate lower voltage.
  • Idle draw: Jackery lists 25 W standby for the 2000 Plus. EcoFlow support has quoted 23 W with AC on and 5 W with AC off for the DELTA 2 Max; The Solar Lab measured about 13.6 W. At CPAP and router loads that is the biggest runtime factor.
  • Switchover: DELTA 2 Max within 30 ms (user manual); Explorer 2000 Plus 20 ms (Jackery calls it EPS); HomePower 2000 Plus v2 10 ms.
  • Expansion: DELTA 2 Max up to two extra batteries, 6,144 Wh. Explorer 2000 Plus up to five Battery Pack 2000 Plus units, about 12 kWh, and two units can run in parallel. The v2 also takes five packs (about 12 kWh) but its packs are a different model and it does not run in parallel.
  • Our pick: DELTA 2 Max for most 120 V outage and camping use at $1,029. Explorer 2000 Plus for a 30 A RV hookup, two 1,500 W loads at once with headroom, or a 240 V pair. If you want the Jackery ecosystem new, wait for the HomePower 2000 Plus v2 restock (pre-order ships November 30).

What happened to the Explorer 2000 Plus

On November 15, 2026, Jackery's Explorer 2000 Plus product link answered with a permanent (301) redirect to the HomePower 2000 Plus v2. Jackery says the v2 is 21 lb lighter than its predecessor and rates its cells for 6,000 cycles. It is not a straight upgrade, though:

  • Output drops from 3,000 W to 2,400 W, and the 30 A TT-30R outlet is gone. On paper the v2 is a DELTA 2 Max competitor, not a 2000 Plus replacement for RV users.
  • Batteries do not carry over. Jackery's store page says the original Battery Pack 2000 Plus is not compatible with the v2, and the v2 takes only the Battery Pack 2000 Plus v2. If you already own 2000 Plus packs, the original unit is the only head that uses them.
  • No parallel or 240 V. Jackery's v2 table lists parallel operation as not supported; the original 2000 Plus supports it.
  • Better at small loads: standby drops from 25 W to 16 W or less, switchover from 20 ms to 10 ms, and USB-C rises to 140 W. Solar input falls from 1,400 W to 800 W.

Original Explorer 2000 Plus units may still be listed by third-party sellers. Compare any offer against the DELTA 2 Max at $1,029 new: the 2000 Plus is worth more only for its 3,000 W, TT-30R and parallel features.

What 600 W more output actually buys

A 2,400 W inverter covers one big load at a time: a microwave, a space heater, a window AC or a sump pump. The 2000 Plus's 3,000 W matters when two of them overlap. Running watts come from our appliance records (maker manuals and labels):

Motor starts: our sump pump guide checks both units against Honda's 1/3 and 1/2 HP starting-watt chart (1,300 W and 2,150 W), and both clear it alone. The difference shows when a pump or compressor starts while something else is already running: 6,000 W of surge leaves room for a start on top of a 1,500 W load, and 4,800 W leaves less. EcoFlow's X-Boost does not help here; the manual says it suits heating devices and is unavailable while the unit is recharging in bypass mode.

The TT-30R: the Explorer 2000 Plus has a 120 V 30 A RV outlet, so a travel trailer's shore cord plugs straight in. A DELTA 2 Max needs a 15-to-30 A adapter and is limited by its 2,400 W rating either way. Our RV guide explains why a 13,500 Btu/h roof AC is still a stretch for both.

Idle draw decides small-load runtime

With the same usable energy, the only thing that separates these boxes on a long, light load is what the inverter burns to stay on. Jackery publishes a figure for both of its units: 25 W standby for the Explorer 2000 Plus and 16 W or less for the v2 (2000 Plus table, v2 table). EcoFlow does not publish one on its spec page. Its support team told one owner the DELTA 2 Max draws 23 W with AC on and 5 W with AC off (diglloyd, quoting EcoFlow support), while The Solar Lab measured about 13.6 W with the inverter on and nothing plugged in. What it means: on a 7 W CPAP with the humidifier off, the spread runs from about 2.3 days on the 2000 Plus to 3.5 days on a DELTA 2 Max that idles like The Solar Lab's unit. On a fridge-router-CPAP mix, about 10% separates best from worst. At window-AC and heater loads, idle stops mattering and both are about the same. Whichever you buy, turn AC off when nothing needs it and run DC loads from the 12 V port: EcoFlow's own 5 W AC-off figure shows how much of that idle is the inverter.

Charging: wall, solar and car

  • Wall: both pull about 1,800 W from a 120 V, 15 A outlet. The DELTA 2 Max reached full in about 80 minutes in independent testing (EcoFlow's 43-minute claim is for 0–80% with AC and solar combined). Jackery lists about 2 hours for the 2000 Plus and 98 minutes for the v2 in emergency mode.
  • Solar: the 2000 Plus takes the most, 1,400 W across two 60 V ports (up to six panels, matched per port). The DELTA 2 Max takes 1,000 W (500 W per port, 11–60 V, 15 A). The v2 takes 800 W, and its two ports are wired in parallel, so panels must match and three panels are not supported. In winter, size panels for your city with SolarSized; even 1,000 W of panels rarely refills 2 kWh in a short December day.
  • Pass-through limits: while plugged into the wall, the DELTA 2 Max bypass carries up to 15 A but drops to 12 A after 3 hours (manual). Jackery lists 12 A for the 2000 Plus in simultaneous charge and discharge, and the same 15 A / 12 A-after-3 h rule for the v2. Treat 1,440 W as the safe pass-through number on all three.
  • Car: all three accept 12 V car charging at 8 A (about 100 W). That is roughly 20–25 hours for a full charge (Jackery lists about 25 h for the 2000 Plus), so treat it as a top-up while driving, not a plan.

For a three-day outage on battery alone (about 6–7 kWh for a fridge, CPAP, router and lights), the DELTA 2 Max tops out right at the edge with two extra batteries, while either Jackery head has room to keep going. For 240 V loads (a well pump, a dryer, a central AC condenser), a single DELTA 2 Max or v2 is out; two original 2000 Plus units in parallel are an option, but at that point compare a purpose-built 240 V unit in our F3800 Plus vs DELTA Pro 3 guide or a 120/240 V generator in the house generator guide.

CPAP, oxygen and other medical loads

Medical safety notice: Will It Run? gives general power estimates only, not medical advice. Before relying on any power station for a medical device, confirm approved power sources with the device maker and your doctor or equipment supplier, and test your setup before an emergency. If your therapy is life-sustaining, keep the backup your supplier provides, register with your utility's medical-needs program, and have an evacuation plan. In an emergency, call 911.

Any of the three runs a home CPAP for several nights. On AC, an AirSense 11 at its typical 56.1 W with the humidifier on gets about ~2.8 nights on a DELTA 2 Max at EcoFlow's 23 W idle, ~2.7 nights on the Explorer 2000 Plus and ~3.0 nights on the v2. With the humidifier off (about 7 W) that becomes ~7.3 nights, ~6.8 nights and ~9.5 nights. All three have a 12 V, 10 A car socket, so a maker-approved DC converter skips the inverter. On DC, a best case of usable Wh × 0.85 ÷ (7 W + about 5 W standby) is roughly ~15.4 nights on either 2 kWh unit. EcoFlow's 5 W AC-off figure is the only standby number either maker has given, and real converters lose more. Switchover matters if the CPAP stays plugged in through the station all the time: 10 ms on the v2, 20 ms on the 2000 Plus and up to 30 ms on the DELTA 2 Max. Test by pulling the wall plug while the machine runs. Commissions play no part in these compatibility notes. Our CPAP power station guide covers converters and humidifier settings, and most CPAP users need a much smaller, cheaper station than either of these. For concentrators, see the oxygen concentrator guide.

Which one to buy

  • EcoFlow DELTA 2 Max ($1,029, in stock): 120 V essentials during outages (fridge, CPAP, router, lights, a microwave in turns), car camping, and anyone who has to carry it. Expandable to 6 kWh. Turn AC off between uses.
  • Jackery Explorer 2000 Plus (if you find a fair price): you want a 30 A TT-30R for a trailer, need two 1,500 W loads or a motor start on top of a big load, own 2000 Plus battery packs already, or plan to pair two units for 240 V. Accept 61.5 lb and the 25 W standby.

Questions

Which is better, the EcoFlow DELTA 2 Max or the Jackery Explorer 2000 Plus?

For most 120 V backup and camping, the DELTA 2 Max: same usable energy, 11.5 lb lighter, $1,029 in stock at EcoFlow on November 15, 2026. The Explorer 2000 Plus wins on output (3,000 W vs 2,400 W), has a 30 A TT-30R outlet, expands to about 12 kWh and supports parallel use, but Jackery's US store now redirects it to the HomePower 2000 Plus v2.

Is the Jackery Explorer 2000 Plus discontinued?

Jackery's US product link for the Explorer 2000 Plus redirected to the HomePower 2000 Plus v2 on November 15, 2026. The v2 has 2,048 Wh, 2,400 W, 10 ms switchover and 16 W or less standby, but no TT-30R, no parallel operation, and it does not accept the original Battery Pack 2000 Plus.

Which runs a CPAP longer?

On AC, idle draw decides it. With an AirSense 11 at 56.1 W: DELTA 2 Max about 22 h at EcoFlow's 23 W idle, Explorer 2000 Plus about 21 h at Jackery's 25 W, HomePower 2000 Plus v2 about 24 h at 16 W. All three have a 12 V 10 A car socket for a DC converter, which avoids inverter idle.

Can the DELTA 2 Max run two 1,500 W appliances at once?

No. Two 1,500 W loads total 3,000 W, over its 2,400 W rating. X-Boost (3,400 W) lowers voltage for heating elements and is not a way to run two full loads. The Explorer 2000 Plus is rated exactly 3,000 W, so it has no headroom either; stagger the loads.

How far can each one expand?

The DELTA 2 Max takes two extra batteries for 6,144 Wh. The Explorer 2000 Plus and the HomePower 2000 Plus v2 each take five of their own battery packs, about 12 kWh, but the packs are different models and not interchangeable.

What is the switchover time for UPS use?

EcoFlow's DELTA 2 Max manual says it switches to battery within 30 ms. Jackery lists 20 ms for the Explorer 2000 Plus (which it calls EPS) and 10 ms for the HomePower 2000 Plus v2. None is a 0 ms online UPS; test your device before relying on it.
