Anker SOLIX F3800 Plus vs EcoFlow DELTA Pro 3: which 240 V home battery fits your house

The Anker SOLIX F3800 Plus puts out 6,000 W at 120/240 V and takes up to six 3.84 kWh batteries; the EcoFlow DELTA Pro 3 stores a little more (4,096 Wh), weighs 23 lb less and switches over faster (10 ms), but tops out at 4,000 W. Pick the F3800 Plus for 240 V loads above 4 kW, a 3/4 HP 3-wire well pump, or a 240 V generator you want to run through the station. Pick the DELTA Pro 3 if 4,000 W covers your list and you want the lighter, faster-switching unit. On November 12, 2026 both were listed as Sold Out on the makers' US stores (F3800 Plus $2,999.99; DELTA Pro 3 $2,799, compare-at $3,299).

Short answer

  • Output: F3800 Plus 6,000 W continuous (L14-30R at 25 A); DELTA Pro 3 4,000 W (L14-30R and 6-20R at 16.7 A). EcoFlow's 6,000 W X-Boost only helps heating-element loads that tolerate lower voltage, not motors.
  • Motor starts at 240 V: 9,000 W ÷ 240 V = 37.5 A for the F3800 (original F3800 figure) vs 8,000 W ÷ 240 V ≈ 33.3 A for the DELTA Pro 3. That is the difference between "thin margin" and "borderline" on a 3/4 HP 3-wire well pump.
  • Idle draw: Anker publishes about 40 W with AC on and nothing connected. EcoFlow publishes no idle figure; independent reports range from about 15–18 W to 30–50 W. For CPAP-sized loads, idle is as big as the machine on either unit.
  • Expansion: F3800 Plus up to 6 × BP3800 = 26,880 Wh on one unit; DELTA Pro 3 up to 2 extra batteries = about 12 kWh per unit (36 kWh across three units with a Smart Home Panel 2).
  • Generator: the F3800 Plus passes a 240 V generator through to its L14-30R (up to 6,000 W). The DELTA Pro 3's AC bypass is 120 V, 15 A (12 A continuous).
  • Our pick: F3800 Plus for 240 V-heavy homes and well pumps; DELTA Pro 3 for 120 V-heavy backup where weight and the 10 ms UPS matter. Below about 2,000 W of 120 V loads, neither: a 2 kWh station costs about a third as much.

Idle draw decides small-load runtime

A big split-phase inverter burns power just staying on. Anker's FAQ for the F3800 Plus is unusually candid: "With AC on and no devices connected, F3800 Plus consumes about 40W," with an idle discharge of about 95 hours (Anker FAQ). EcoFlow does not publish a DELTA Pro 3 figure. An Australian review measured about 15–18 W idle on its 230 V unit (I'mTech review), while DELTA Pro 3 owners on r/Ecoflow_community report 30–50 W (owner thread). What it means: at heater and air-conditioner loads the idle figure barely matters, and the F3800's lower capacity puts it about 6% behind. At CPAP, router and single-fridge loads the DELTA Pro 3 could run anywhere from roughly even with the F3800 to more than twice as long, depending on which idle figure is right for your unit. Either way, the fix is the same: turn AC off when nothing needs it, and run small loads from DC. The F3800's 12 V car socket takes a CPAP DC converter directly; the DELTA Pro 3's 12 V output is an Anderson port, so you would need an Anderson-to-socket adapter that EcoFlow does not list for medical use.

240 V loads and well pumps

Most deep-well submersibles are 240 V motors that pull locked-rotor amps for a fraction of a second at start. Divide a station's surge watts by 240 V to compare it with the motor table. Franklin Electric's 4-inch motor data (used in our well pump guide) gives this paper check:

Paper estimate, not a bench test: it treats surge watts as volt-amps and ignores how long each inverter holds surge. The 9,000 W figure is Anker's published surge for the original F3800; the F3800 Plus spec list gives none. EcoFlow's blog claims the DELTA Pro 3 can start a 1 HP pump; we have not seen a test.

Running power is not the issue: a 1 HP pump runs at about 1,210 W, well inside either unit. The start is. A soft-start pump such as the Grundfos SQ changes the math for both, and a 120/240 V generator remains the safer answer for 1 HP and larger motors.

Other 240 V differences: the DELTA Pro 3 outputs either 120 V or 240 V, not both. In 240 V mode its 120 V outlets go dark, although a panel interlock still feeds 120 V circuits on both legs. The F3800 Plus supplies both on battery, but its L14-30R shuts off while it recharges from a 120 V wall outlet. Only the three UPS outlets and the TT-30R stay live then, with a 1,440 W bypass limit. For central AC, EcoFlow says one DELTA Pro 3 can power a 3-ton unit; at 4,000 W total that leaves little headroom for anything else while the compressor runs, and the start is the real test. The F3800's 6,000 W leaves more room for the blower, fridge and lights. Check the condenser's RLA and LRA on its nameplate either way.

Charging and generators

  • Wall: both pull about 1,800 W from a 120 V, 15 A outlet, so a flat battery takes roughly 2.5 hours or a little more. EcoFlow lists 100–240 V input for the DELTA Pro 3; Anker lists 120 V for the F3800 Plus wall inlet.
  • Generator pass-through: this is the F3800 Plus's headline change. A 240 V pure-sine generator (THD under 5%) on its Home Power Panel port passes up to 6,000 W to the L14-30R. A 120 V generator feeds only the TT-30R at up to 3,000 W, with 30 ms and 50 ms switchover. The DELTA Pro 3 charges from a generator through its AC input and can auto-start EcoFlow's Smart Generator 4000. EcoFlow says it keeps 4,000 W of 120/240 V output while pass-through charging.
  • Solar: the F3800 Plus takes 3,200 W across two 165 V ports, so three 400–500 W panels per port in series. The DELTA Pro 3 takes 2,600 W: 1,600 W high-voltage (30–150 V) plus 1,000 W low-voltage (11–60 V). In December sun, either still needs most of a day to refill. Size panels for your city with SolarSized.
  • Car: both accept 12 V car charging at about 100–120 W, which is a trickle for a 4 kWh battery. EcoFlow lists 48 V, 20 A car input for its alternator charger setups.

If you are planning a three-day outage on battery alone (about 6–7 kWh for a fridge, CPAP, router and lights), the F3800 Plus with one BP3800 or the DELTA Pro 3 with one extra battery both get there. Past about 12 kWh on a single inverter, only the Anker can keep stacking.

CPAP, oxygen and other medical loads

Medical safety notice: Will It Run? gives general power estimates only, not medical advice. Before relying on any power station for a medical device, confirm approved power sources with the device maker and your doctor or equipment supplier, and test your setup before an emergency. If your therapy is life-sustaining, keep the backup your supplier provides, register with your utility's medical-needs program, and have an evacuation plan. In an emergency, call 911.

Both units run any home CPAP or BiPAP for at least a day, and most for several. The decision points are switchover time and idle draw, not capacity. EcoFlow publishes 10 ms; Anker publishes 20 ms on the F3800's three UPS outlets, and only those outlets are on UPS. Test either one by pulling the wall plug while the machine runs. On the F3800, a ResMed DC converter in the 12 V socket skips the 40 W inverter idle entirely. An AirSense 11 with the humidifier off goes from about 2.9 days on AC to about 19 days on DC. Commissions play no part in these compatibility notes. Our CPAP power station guide covers converters, humidifier settings and smaller, cheaper stations that suit most CPAP users better than either of these. Which one to buy

  • Anker SOLIX F3800 Plus: you have a 240 V well pump up to 3/4 HP 3-wire, want 240 V and 120 V at the same time, plan to run a 240 V generator through the station, or need more than about 12 kWh on one inverter. Accept the weight (136.7 lb) and the 40 W idle, and keep CPAP-type loads on DC.
  • EcoFlow DELTA Pro 3: your 240 V loads stay under 4,000 W (a 1/2 HP 3-wire pump, a dryer on a timer, an EV top-up), you want the 10 ms switchover, and you will move the unit around. At 113.5 lb it is still a two-person lift on stairs.
  • Neither: everything you need is 120 V and under 2,000 W. A DELTA 2 Max or Explorer 2000 v2 does that for about a third of the price, and two of them still cost less than one of these.
  • Timing: both were sold out at the makers on November 12, 2026. Don't pay over list at third-party sellers; recheck after the Black Friday sales end.

Questions

Which is better, the Anker F3800 Plus or the EcoFlow DELTA Pro 3?

For 240 V-heavy homes, the F3800 Plus: 6,000 W versus 4,000 W, a higher surge figure for well pumps, 240 V generator pass-through, and up to 26.9 kWh on one unit. For mostly 120 V backup, the DELTA Pro 3: 23 lb lighter, a 10 ms switchover versus 20 ms, slightly more capacity (4,096 Wh versus 3,840 Wh).

Which runs a CPAP longer?

On AC it depends on idle draw. Anker lists about 40 W for the F3800 Plus; EcoFlow publishes none for the DELTA Pro 3, and reports range from about 15 to 50 W. With an AirSense 11 at 56.1 W: F3800 about 34 h; DELTA Pro 3 about 36–49 h. On the F3800's 12 V car socket with a ResMed DC converter, there is no inverter idle at all.

Can either one run a 1 HP well pump?

Not with margin on paper. The F3800's 9,000 W surge works out to 37.5 A at 240 V and the DELTA Pro 3's 8,000 W to about 33.3 A. A standard 1 HP 3-wire motor pulls about 41.8 A locked-rotor (Franklin). A soft-start pump or a 120/240 V generator is the safer plan.

Can the DELTA Pro 3 output 120 V and 240 V at the same time?

No. EcoFlow's FAQ says it runs in 120 V or 240 V mode. In 240 V mode the L14-30R and 6-20R are live and the 120 V outlets are off, though a panel interlock still feeds 120 V circuits on both legs.

How much can each one expand?

The F3800 Plus takes up to six BP3800 batteries, 26,880 Wh on one unit. The DELTA Pro 3 takes up to two extra batteries, about 12 kWh per unit, or 36 kWh across three units on a Smart Home Panel 2.

Are they in stock?

On November 12, 2026 both makers' US stores listed them as sold out: the F3800 Plus at $2,999.99 and the DELTA Pro 3 at $2,799 (compare-at $3,299). Prices change often; check current listings before you buy.
