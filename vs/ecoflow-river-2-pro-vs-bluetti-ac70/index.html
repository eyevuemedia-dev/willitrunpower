EcoFlow RIVER 2 Pro vs BLUETTI AC70: output, solar, UPS and the EB70

Same 768 Wh LiFePO4 battery, so the same runtime. The BLUETTI AC70 does more: 1,000 W instead of 800 W, a published 1,500 W surge that applies to motors, 500 W solar instead of 220 W, a 20 ms UPS instead of 30 ms, and add-on batteries. The EcoFlow RIVER 2 Pro is 4.3 lb lighter, has 4 AC outlets instead of 2, charges just as fast from the wall, and was $369 and in stock at EcoFlow US on November 18, 2026. The AC70 was listed at $359 but sold out at BLUETTI. Buy the AC70 for a small fridge, solar or room to grow, if you can find it at a fair price. Buy the RIVER 2 Pro for camping, a CPAP and electronics.

Short answer

  • Runtime is a tie on paper. Both hold 768 Wh, 653 Wh usable in our math. Idle draw on AC is the real difference: BLUETTI publishes about 15 W for the AC70; EcoFlow publishes nothing, and one lab measured 3.2 W on a 230 V RIVER 2 Pro.
  • Output: AC70 1,000 W continuous with a 1,500 W surge (BLUETTI spec). RIVER 2 Pro 800 W; EcoFlow's spec line says "surge 1,600 W" but its comparison table calls the same figure X-Boost, so we don't use it for motor starts.
  • Charging: a tie from the wall (940 W vs 850–950 W, both about 70–90 minutes to full). Solar is not close: 500 W on the AC70 vs 220 W on the RIVER 2 Pro.
  • Switchover: AC70 ≤20 ms UPS (manual). RIVER 2 Pro <30 ms "EPS". Test either one with your own device.
  • Portability: RIVER 2 Pro 18.2 lb vs AC70 22.5 lb. RIVER 2 Pro has 4 AC outlets and Wi-Fi + Bluetooth; AC70 has 2 AC outlets and Bluetooth only.
  • Our pick: AC70 for home backup with a small fridge or solar, if it is in stock near $359. RIVER 2 Pro at $369 for camping, CPAP and electronics, and as the one you can actually buy today.

RIVER 2 Pro vs EB70: the RIVER 2 Pro is lighter, a bit bigger in capacity, and refills from the wall in about 70 minutes instead of 4 hours. That last point matters most in a rolling outage or on a short generator run. The EB70 keeps the wireless pad and more DC ports, which some campers like. If you already own an EB70, it does the same job for a CPAP or lights. There is no runtime reason to replace it; upgrade only if the slow 200 W charging is a real problem. Buying new? The AC70 is BLUETTI's replacement in this size, and BLUETTI also lists an AC70P (1,000 W, 864 Wh, $469 and sold out on November 18).

Output, surge and motor loads

For electronics, lights, a CPAP, a TV or a laptop, both are far more than enough. The extra 200 W only matters for kitchen gear and anything with a motor.

  • Compressors and pumps: we only count a surge figure for motor starts when the maker presents it as surge. BLUETTI does (1,500 W), so the AC70 has some headroom to start a compact fridge. Most fridge makers don't publish startup watts, though, so test yours. EcoFlow's comparison table lists the RIVER 2 Pro's 1,600 W as X-Boost, a voltage-lowering mode for heaters, so we don't clear compressors on it from published data. Plenty of owners run small fridges on one; test yours before an outage.
  • Sump pumps: neither one. A Zoeller M53 runs at about 1,116 W, above both continuous ratings (AC70 + Zoeller M53). See best power station for a sump pump.
  • Heaters, kettles, hair dryers: both boost modes let a 1,200–1,500 W resistive load run at reduced voltage, so it runs but puts out less heat. BLUETTI's manual says Power Lifting is for resistive loads of 1,000–2,000 W and that the real output stays at 1,000 W. On 768 Wh, a heater is a 40-minute load at best; see power stations for space heaters.
  • Microwaves: check the label's input watts, not cooking watts. A 1,100 W-output Panasonic NN-SU656 draws 1,500 W, which is over both (RIVER 2 Pro + Panasonic microwave). Only a small microwave rated under 1,000 W input fits the AC70, and none over 800 W fits the RIVER 2 Pro. Ken Rockwell's AC70 review ran a 1,475 W corn popper briefly in Power Lifting and saw a 1,650 W hair dryer shut it down.

Runtimes and idle draw

With the same 768 Wh, runtimes match on paper. We use 653 Wh usable and add about 15 W on AC for the inverter's own draw. For the AC70 that is BLUETTI's published self-consumption, and Ken Rockwell measured about the same. EcoFlow doesn't publish an idle figure. Best Batteries measured a 230 V RIVER 2 Pro at 3.2 W with AC on and no load (0.4 W with AC off). We haven't seen a 120 V measurement, so the table shows both.

Our math: 653 Wh ÷ (load + idle). BLUETTI's formula is more conservative: 768 Wh × 90% DoD × 85% ÷ (load + 15 W). Its own example is a 40 W fridge for about 10.7 h. The 3.2 W column is a single lab test on the European model, not an EcoFlow spec.

Two practical points. First, small overnight loads are where idle draw matters: a no-humidifier CPAP at 7 W uses less than half of what a 15 W inverter burns just staying on. Second, both makers ship power-saving timers that switch AC off at low load. BLUETTI's ECO mode turns outputs off under 40 W by default, and its manual says to disable ECO for small devices. Check this before you rely on either unit for a CPAP or router overnight.

Charging and solar

  • Wall: close to a tie. EcoFlow lists 940 W and 0–100% in 70 minutes. BLUETTI lists 950 W Turbo on the product page (1.3–1.6 h) and 850 W in the manual (80% in 45 minutes, full in 1.5 h in Turbo, 2 h in Standard). Turbo is set in the app; Silent mode (4 h, 45 dB) is the quiet overnight option.
  • Solar: the AC70 takes up to 500 W at 12–58 V, so one 350 W panel or two 200 W panels in series work, and BLUETTI quotes 2.8–3.3 hours to full. The RIVER 2 Pro takes 220 W at 11–50 V: a single 220 W panel, and in practice most of a sunny day to refill. For multi-day outages on solar this is the biggest functional difference; see SolarSized to size panels for your location.
  • Car: both charge from a 12/24 V socket. EcoFlow lists 8 A (100 W max on 12 V), so about 8 hours for a full charge.
  • Generator: both charge from any clean inverter generator through the AC input. The AC70 is limited to 1,000 W of AC + DC input combined.

UPS switchover: 20 ms vs 30 ms

Both pass grid power through and switch to battery when the grid drops. BLUETTI's manual lists a switching time of 20 ms or less for the AC70; EcoFlow lists under 30 ms and calls it EPS. Neither is an online UPS, and BLUETTI's manual says it is not liable for damage when the unit powers devices that need a high-performance UPS, naming servers, workstations and medical equipment. Routers, lamps, fridges and most laptops won't notice either gap. A desktop PC may or may not ride through 20–30 ms depending on its power supply. Pull the wall plug while it is running to find out.

Expansion

The RIVER 2 Pro is fixed at 768 Wh. The AC70 can use BLUETTI's B80 (806 Wh), B230 (2,048 Wh) or B300 (3,072 Wh) in Power Bank mode. In that mode the add-on battery charges the AC70 through its DC input; it is not a true parallel expansion port. With a B80, BLUETTI quotes 1,574 Wh total and sells it as a kit ($898, sold out on Nov 18). If you might want 1.5–3 kWh later, this is a real point for the AC70. If you know you need more than about 1 kWh, start with a larger unit instead: see EcoFlow vs Jackery vs BLUETTI or the 24-hour outage plan.

CPAP and medical devices

Both have a 10 A 12 V car outlet, so both work with ResMed's DC/DC converters, which skip the inverter. On 653 Wh usable, a ResMed AirSense 11 (ResMed power table) gets roughly:

On AC, the AC70's faster switchover (20 ms vs 30 ms) is a small plus for a bedside unit left plugged in, but neither maker promises ride-through for a medical device. Medical safety notice: Will It Run? gives general power estimates only, not medical advice. Power draw varies by model, settings, pressure, humidifier and altitude. Before relying on any battery, power station, inverter or generator for a medical device, confirm approved power sources with the device manufacturer and your doctor or equipment supplier, and test your setup before an emergency. Never change prescribed settings to extend battery life. If your therapy is life-sustaining, keep the backup your supplier provides, register with your utility's medical-needs program, and have an evacuation plan. Run generators outdoors only, at least 20 ft from windows and doors, because of carbon monoxide risk. In an emergency, call 911.

Which to buy

  • Buy the BLUETTI AC70 for home backup with a compact fridge, for solar (500 W), or if you might add a battery later. BLUETTI listed it at $359 but sold out on November 18. Don't overpay a reseller: once it costs more than a RIVER 2 Pro, the 1 kWh units above are better value.
  • Buy the EcoFlow RIVER 2 Pro ($369 and in stock at EcoFlow on November 18) for camping, a CPAP on the DC converter, laptops and electronics. You also get four AC outlets and the lighter box. Skip it if you need to start a compressor or want more than 220 W of solar.
  • Own an EB70 or EB70S? Keep it for the same jobs; the main thing you would gain is charging speed.

Questions

Which is better, the EcoFlow RIVER 2 Pro or the BLUETTI AC70?

They hold the same 768 Wh. The AC70 has more output (1,000 W vs 800 W), a published 1,500 W surge for motors, 500 W solar input vs 220 W, a 20 ms UPS vs 30 ms, and add-on batteries. The RIVER 2 Pro is 4.3 lb lighter, has four AC outlets instead of two, and was $369 and in stock at EcoFlow on November 18, 2026, while the AC70 ($359) was sold out at BLUETTI.

Which runs longer?

Neither, on paper: both are 768 Wh, about 653 Wh usable in our math. On AC, idle draw decides it. BLUETTI publishes about 15 W self-consumption for the AC70. EcoFlow publishes no figure; one lab measured 3.2 W on a 230 V RIVER 2 Pro. For small overnight loads like a CPAP, that gap can be worth hours.

BLUETTI EB70 vs EcoFlow RIVER 2 Pro: which should I get?

The RIVER 2 Pro. It holds 768 Wh vs 716 Wh, weighs 18.2 lb vs 21.4 lb, has 3,000 vs 2,500 rated cycles, and charges from the wall at 940 W (about 70 minutes) instead of the EB70's 200 W adapter (4 to 4.5 hours). The EB70 has a wireless charging pad and more DC ports. The EB70S is the 800 W version of the 700 W EB70; it was sold out at BLUETTI on November 18, 2026.

Can the AC70 or RIVER 2 Pro run a refrigerator?

The AC70 has a published 1,500 W surge, which gives some headroom for a compact fridge start; most fridge makers don't publish startup watts, so test yours. A Danby compact fridge averaging about 25 W runs about 16 hours on either unit with our 15 W idle allowance. EcoFlow lists the RIVER 2 Pro's 1,600 W as X-Boost, so we can't clear compressor starts on it from published data; test your fridge first.

Can I add a battery to either one?

Only the AC70. It accepts BLUETTI's B80, B230 or B300 in Power Bank mode, where the extra battery charges the AC70 through its DC input (AC70 + B80 = 1,574 Wh). The RIVER 2 Pro is fixed at 768 Wh.

Which is better for a CPAP?

For runtime, they tie. A no-humidifier ResMed AirSense 11 (about 7 W) gets roughly 11.7 nights on either unit through ResMed's DC converter and the 12 V car outlet, or about 3.7 nights on AC with a 15 W idle allowance. With a heated tube (about 55 W) it is about 1.5 vs 1.2 nights. Turn off BLUETTI's ECO mode for small loads, test your setup, and confirm power sources with your device maker.
