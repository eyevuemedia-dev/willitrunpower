EcoFlow DELTA 2 vs Anker SOLIX C1000: plus the C1000 Gen 2 and DELTA 3

For runtime it's a wash: 1,056 Wh in the Anker vs 1,024 Wh in the EcoFlow, about 3% more. The Anker SOLIX C1000 has the edge on paper: 600 W solar vs 500 W, a 20 ms UPS vs 30 ms, and faster wall charging. The DELTA 2 has more surge (2,700 W vs 2,400 W). It was $479.99 and in stock at Anker US on November 18, 2026. The DELTA 2 is now hard to buy new: EcoFlow's US store redirects it to the DELTA 3 ($579, in stock), which beats both originals on UPS (<10 ms) and surge (3,600 W). If you want the newest Anker, the C1000 Gen 2 ($529.99, pre-order shipping early December) adds 2,000 W, a 10 ms UPS and 49-minute charging but drops the expansion battery.

Short answer

  • Runtime: C1000 898 Wh usable in our math vs 870 Wh for the DELTA 2, DELTA 3 and C1000 Gen 2. That works out to about 3% more on the C1000. You won't notice it.
  • UPS switchover: DELTA 2 within 30 ms ("basic UPS"). C1000 under 20 ms. C1000 Gen 2 and DELTA 3 under 10 ms. For a desktop PC or a bedside CPAP left on AC, the 10 ms pair is the safer bet.
  • Surge: DELTA 2 2,700 W, C1000 2,400 W (SurgePad), C1000 Gen 2 3,000 W peak, DELTA 3 3,600 W. None of this replaces your appliance's published startup watts.
  • Charging: DELTA 2 1,200 W (80 min) · C1000 1,300 W (58 min) · Gen 2 1,600 W (49 min, above 68 °F) · DELTA 3 1,500 W. Solar: 500 / 600 / 600 / 500 W.
  • Expansion: DELTA 2 to about 3 kWh, C1000 to 2,112 Wh, DELTA 3 to 5 kWh. The C1000 Gen 2 can't be expanded.
  • Our pick: the C1000 at $479.99 for the best value and solar. The DELTA 3 at $579 if UPS speed or motor surge matters most. The C1000 Gen 2 if you want the lightest 2,000 W box and will never add a battery.

What happened to these models

  • EcoFlow DELTA 2: on November 17 and 18, 2026, EcoFlow's US store redirected the DELTA 2 product link to the DELTA 3. We found no discontinuation notice. The informational DELTA 2 page is still up, and new units now come mainly from third-party sellers and clearance stock. See DELTA 3 Plus vs DELTA 2 (and DELTA 3).
  • Anker SOLIX C1000: Anker's C1000 product URL now opens a C1000X listing (model A1761111): $599.99 list, $479.99 after an auto-applied $120 code on November 18. Its spec table matches the C1000: 1,056 Wh, 1,800 W, 2,400 W surge, 1,300 W AC input, under 20 ms UPS, 28.44 lb, expandable to 2,112 Wh. Our numbers below use that table.
  • Anker SOLIX C1000 Gen 2: a lighter redesign, not a bigger battery. It has 1,024 Wh instead of 1,056 Wh, 2,000 W, a 10 ms UPS, 1,600 W charging, two 140 W USB-C ports and 4,000 cycles. Anker's FAQ says it "does not include an expansion feature" and recommends the original C1000 if you need more capacity. On November 18 the US page said orders placed after that day ship in early December.

UPS switchover: 30, 20 or 10 ms

All four pass grid power through and switch to battery when the grid drops. EcoFlow's DELTA 2 manual says within 30 ms, calls it a basic UPS function and says it does not support 0 ms switching. Anker lists the C1000 at under 20 ms. The C1000 Gen 2 and EcoFlow's DELTA 3 list under 10 ms.

  • Desktop PCs, NAS boxes, some monitors: 10 ms is the safest. Some PC power supplies ride through 20–30 ms and some don't.
  • Routers, fridges, lamps: any of them; these loads don't notice.
  • CPAP on AC at the bedside: no maker publishes ride-through for a CPAP. With the CPAP running, unplug the station from the wall and confirm therapy continues.

None of these is an online double-conversion UPS. Buy a dedicated online UPS if a device must never see a gap.

Surge and motor loads

Same 1,800 W running output on the DELTA 2, C1000 and DELTA 3; 2,000 W on the Gen 2. Surge is the short burst for motor starts:

  • DELTA 2: 2,700 W (US manual, 120 V). C1000: 2,400 W. Anker calls it SurgePad, and its FAQ says SurgePad kicks in when output exceeds the rating and works best with heat-generating devices. C1000 Gen 2: 3,000 W peak. DELTA 3: 3,600 W.
  • We only clear a motor start when the appliance maker publishes startup watts. Most fridge, window-AC and sump-pump makers don't, so these verdicts stay "runs, surge risk." Examples: C1000 + 12,000 Btu window AC (800 W running, about 1.1 h), DELTA 2 + Danby compact fridge, DELTA 2 + Zoeller M53 sump pump.
  • If a motor load is at the edge, the DELTA 3's 3,600 W gives the most margin, followed by the Gen 2's 3,000 W peak.

Runtimes

We use 85% of nameplate as usable and add about 15 W for the inverter on AC. That figure is BLUETTI's published self-consumption for its AC180; neither Anker nor EcoFlow publishes one. In OutdoorGearLab's test, the C1000 delivered 897 Wh from its AC outlets, which matches our 898 Wh assumption.

Runtime = usable Wh ÷ (load W + 15 W). DELTA 2, DELTA 3 and C1000 Gen 2 share 1,024 Wh, so they share a column.

The idle term is why small overnight loads look short on AC. A 7 W CPAP uses less than half of what an inverter spends just staying on. Turn AC off when you don't need it, use DC outputs where you can, and turn off any power-saving auto-off timer for loads that cycle, like a fridge.

Charging and solar

  • Wall: the Gen 2 is fastest at 49 minutes with UltraFast on in the Anker app, but Anker says this needs cells above 20 °C (68 °F) and is much slower when they are colder. Next is the C1000 at 58 minutes, then the DELTA 3 at 1,500 W and the DELTA 2 at 80 minutes. Any of them refills in 50–80 minutes before a forecast storm.
  • Solar: the Anker pair takes 600 W (11–60 V; Anker quotes 1.8 h to full on the Gen 2); the EcoFlow pair takes 500 W. Over a multi-day outage, 100 W more input adds a few hundred Wh per sunny day. Size panels for your location with SolarSized.
  • Car: all four charge from a 12 V socket, slowly (roughly 9–10 hours from empty at about 120 W). EcoFlow sells an 800 W Alternator Charger for the DELTA 3 series.
  • Generator: any clean inverter generator can charge all four through the AC input. The 2,000 W class is enough; see what a 2,000 W generator runs.

Expansion

The DELTA 3 goes furthest: up to 5 kWh, and EcoFlow lists the DELTA 2 Extra Battery as compatible through its XT150 cable. The DELTA 2 takes one Smart Extra Battery, for 1–3 kWh total. The C1000 doubles to 2,112 Wh with Anker's expansion battery. The C1000 Gen 2 can't be expanded at all. If a 24-hour or 3-day outage plan is the goal, start from the 24-hour and 3-day load plans. If you know you need about 2 kWh, compare a 2 kWh station rather than expanding later; see EcoFlow vs Jackery vs BLUETTI.

For a bedside unit on AC, the 10 ms switchover on the Gen 2 and DELTA 3 is a real advantage over the DELTA 2's 30 ms. Anker markets the Gen 2's 10 ms UPS for CPAP machines, but that is a marketing claim, not a medical rating; test it with your own machine. More: CPAP on DC vs AC, CPAP hub, best power station for a CPAP. Per-machine pages: C1000 + AirSense 11, DELTA 2 + AirSense 11.

Medical safety notice: Will It Run? gives general power estimates only, not medical advice. Power draw varies by model, settings, pressure, humidifier and altitude. Before relying on any battery, power station, inverter or generator for a medical device, confirm approved power sources with the device manufacturer and your doctor or equipment supplier, and test your setup before an emergency. Never change prescribed settings to extend battery life. If your therapy is life-sustaining, keep the backup your supplier provides, register with your utility's medical-needs program, and have an evacuation plan. Run generators outdoors only, at least 20 ft from windows and doors, because of carbon monoxide risk. In an emergency, call 911.

Alternatives

  • EcoFlow DELTA 3 Plus: a DELTA 3 with 1,000 W of solar and 140 W USB-C ($719 on Nov 17).
  • Jackery HomePower 1000 v2: 1,024 Wh and a published standby of 16 W or less, useful for planning small all-night AC loads.
  • BLUETTI AC180: 1,152 Wh, 1,800 W, published 15 W self-consumption. Compare: DELTA 2 vs AC180.
  • Jackery: Explorer 1000 Plus vs C1000 and DELTA 2 vs Explorer 1000 v2.
  • Smaller budget: RIVER 2 Pro vs AC70 (768 Wh) and best power stations under $500.

Which to buy

  • Anker SOLIX C1000 ($479.99, in stock Nov 18): the value pick. It has the most capacity here, 600 W solar, a 20 ms UPS and room to expand to 2 kWh.
  • EcoFlow DELTA 3 ($579): pick it if UPS speed (10 ms) or motor surge (3,600 W) matters most, or you want to grow to 5 kWh. It is the like-for-like replacement for the DELTA 2.
  • Anker SOLIX C1000 Gen 2 ($529.99 pre-order): the lightest box here at 24.9 lb, with 2,000 W, a 10 ms UPS and two 140 W USB-C ports. Skip it if you might ever want an add-on battery.
  • EcoFlow DELTA 2: buy only clearly below a DELTA 3 or C1000, and only if 30 ms switchover is fine for your devices. If you already own one, keep it; the runtime is the same.

Questions

Which is better, the EcoFlow DELTA 2 or the Anker SOLIX C1000?

On paper the C1000: 1,056 vs 1,024 Wh, 600 W vs 500 W solar, under 20 ms vs 30 ms UPS, and 58 vs 80 minutes to charge from the wall. The DELTA 2 has more surge (2,700 W vs 2,400 W) and expands to about 3 kWh vs 2,112 Wh. On November 18, 2026 the C1000 was $479.99 and in stock at Anker US, while EcoFlow's store redirected the DELTA 2 to the DELTA 3 ($579).

Anker SOLIX C1000 Gen 2 vs EcoFlow DELTA 2: what's different?

The Gen 2 has the same 1,024 Wh but 2,000 W output (3,000 W peak) vs 1,800 W (2,700 W surge), a 10 ms vs 30 ms UPS, 1,600 W vs 1,200 W wall charging (49 vs 80 minutes), 600 W vs 500 W solar, 4,000 vs 3,000 rated cycles and 24.9 vs 27 lb. The DELTA 2 can add an extra battery; the Gen 2 cannot. The Gen 2 was $529.99 on pre-order at Anker US on November 18, 2026.

C1000 or C1000 Gen 2?

Get the C1000 ($479.99) if you want the most capacity per dollar or may add a battery (up to 2,112 Wh). Get the Gen 2 ($529.99 pre-order) for 2,000 W output, a 10 ms UPS, two 140 W USB-C ports and a lighter 24.9 lb box. Anker says the Gen 2 has no expansion.

Is the EcoFlow DELTA 2 discontinued?

We found no notice, but on November 17 and 18, 2026 EcoFlow's US store redirected the DELTA 2 product link to the DELTA 3. The DELTA 3 has the same 1,024 Wh and 1,800 W with a 10 ms UPS, 3,600 W surge and 1,500 W charging, for $579.

Which runs a CPAP longer?

Close to a tie. A no-humidifier ResMed AirSense 11 (about 7 W) gets roughly 16.0 nights on the C1000 and 15.5 on the 1,024 Wh units through ResMed's DC converter, or about 5.1 and 4.9 nights on AC with a 15 W idle allowance. For bedside AC use, a 10 ms switchover (C1000 Gen 2, DELTA 3) is better than 20 or 30 ms. Confirm power sources with your device maker.

Can either run a refrigerator?

Both run a fridge's running watts easily; a compact Danby fridge averaging about 25 W gets about 22 hours on AC. Starting a compressor depends on startup watts that most fridge makers don't publish. The DELTA 2 lists 2,700 W surge and the C1000 2,400 W, so test your fridge's start on the station before an outage.
