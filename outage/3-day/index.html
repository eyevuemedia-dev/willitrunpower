3-day power outage backup plan: how much battery, solar or fuel you really need

Three days of essentials (CPAP, router, laptop, lights) take about 3,680 Wh; add a full-size fridge and it's about 6,670 Wh, roughly 7,800 Wh of nameplate battery. You can buy that much battery (EcoFlow DELTA Pro 3 plus one extra battery, or a BLUETTI AC200L with two B300s), but for most homes the smarter plan is a 2 kWh-class station recharged every day. In summer, about 580 W of panels does it in all six cities we checked; in a Detroit or Seattle December you'd need roughly two and a half to nearly four times that, so pair the battery with a small inverter generator. Run about 1.6 hours a day at full charge rate and an EU2200i-class generator burns about 1.4 gallons over three days, versus roughly 8 gallons running it around the clock.

Step 1: the 72-hour load budget

Most outage calculators stop at "watts × hours". Over three days two things dominate that people miss: the fridge, which runs all 72 hours, and the inverter's own draw, about 15 W any time the AC outlets are on (BLUETTI publishes that figure for the AC180; most makers don't publish one). The hybrid usually wins. A small inverter generator is most fuel-efficient near its rated output, and a battery bank lets you run it hard for a short window instead of idling all night for a ~93 W average load. By Honda's own EU2200i figures, a tank lasts 3.2 hours at the rated 1,800 W (6.1 kWh per gallon) but only 8.1 hours at 1/4 load (3.8 kWh per gallon), and a fridge-plus-CPAP house is well under 1/4 load most of the day.

Strategy C: battery + generator recharge

Run the station as the house battery and the generator as its charger. With the fridge budget, the battery needs about 2,610 Wh back each day; we budget another 10% for AC charging losses (makers don't publish charger efficiency, so treat that as our assumption), for about 2,880 Wh a day from the generator.

The catch is matching charge rate to the generator. The EU2200i is rated 1,800 W continuous. The DELTA 2 Max's X-Stream (1,800 W) and the Jackery 2000 Plus on 120 V/15 A land right at that limit, which is why we pick the DELTA 2 Max as a generator partner: EcoFlow's app lets you turn the AC charge rate down to leave headroom for anything else on the generator. The AC200L can pull 2,400 W, more than a 2,200 W-class inverter generator supplies, so use a slower charging mode if its app offers one, or pair it with a bigger generator. The DELTA Pro 3 and F3800 Plus can also take 240 V generator input on larger generators. Let it cool before refueling (Ready.gov), and don't leave it running while you sleep.

Sizing the battery for the hybrid: it has to carry the house from one charge to the next. A 2 kWh station (about 1,740 Wh usable) covers about 19 hours of the fridge budget, so one long charge a day works if you time it for late afternoon, or two shorter runs if you want margin.

Carbon monoxide. The CDC says never run a generator inside a home, basement or garage, even with doors and windows open, and to keep it more than 20 feet from windows, doors and vents. More than 400 Americans die each year from unintentional non-fire CO poisoning. Put battery-powered or battery-backup CO detectors in the house before the outage. A power station has no exhaust and is the only one of these you should run indoors.

Food and medical: what the agencies say

  • Fridge: about 4 hours if the door stays closed; throw out perishables after 4 hours without power (FoodSafety.gov). Three days without a powered fridge means losing its contents.
  • Freezer: about 48 hours full or 24 hours half full with the door closed. A full chest freezer can ride through the first day or two while the battery prioritizes the fridge.
  • Medical devices: make a plan with your medical provider (Ready.gov), and test the setup with the wall power off.
  • Refrigerated medication: Ready.gov says to throw it out if power is out for more than a day unless the label says otherwise. Ask your pharmacist about insulin before an outage, not during.
  • Surges: unplug electronics to avoid damage when power comes back (Ready.gov).

Medical equipment note. This page is general information, not medical advice. If you rely on a CPAP, oxygen concentrator or other powered medical device, confirm your backup plan with your doctor or equipment supplier. Jackery says its power stations are not recommended for medical devices, and EcoFlow says its products are not intended for life-sustaining equipment. Oxygen concentrators draw far more than a CPAP and are not covered by these numbers. Affiliate commissions have no bearing on any compatibility statement here.

A 3-day runbook

  1. Before: charge everything to 100%, freeze water jugs to fill freezer space, test the CO detectors, and do a one-hour dry run with your outage loads on the station.
  2. Hour 0: unplug sensitive electronics, plug the fridge, router and CPAP into the station, and note the battery percentage. Keep fridge and freezer doors shut.
  3. Day 1: log the percentage every few hours (the notepad in the photo isn't decoration). If you're burning more than a third a day, cut loads now, not on day 3.
  4. Each day: recharge at the best time: midday for solar, late afternoon for a generator so the battery is full going into the night.
  5. Night: CPAP from a DC port if you have the cord, which avoids most of the 15 W inverter draw; generator off.
  6. After: check fridge temperature before eating anything, and recharge the bank right away; the next storm doesn't wait.

Questions

How much battery do I need for a 3-day power outage?

For essentials without a fridge (CPAP with humidifier, Wi-Fi router, a laptop and two LED bulbs), about 3,680 Wh over 72 hours including about 15 W of inverter draw, so around 4,300 Wh of nameplate capacity. Add a typical top-freezer fridge and it's about 6,670 Wh, or around 7,800 Wh nameplate. Without any recharging, that means an expandable system with extra batteries.

Can a power station run a refrigerator for 3 days?

Only a big one or one you recharge. A typical top-freezer fridge averages about 41 W (ENERGY STAR median 363 kWh/yr), so 72 hours is about 2,980 Wh plus about 1,080 Wh of inverter draw. A 2 kWh station runs it around 31 hours on its own. The station also needs a published motor-start surge of at least 1,200 W; see our fridge guide.

Is solar enough to get through a 3-day outage?

In sunny months, often yes. With the fridge load above (about 2,220 Wh a day), you need about 580 W of panels at 6 peak sun hours. PVWatts puts Detroit at 2.17 sun hours a day in December and Seattle at 1.46, which pushes that to about 1,600 to 2,400 W, and one overcast day wipes out the plan. Winter outages in the north need a fuel backup.

How much gas does a generator need for 3 days?

It depends on whether it runs all the time. Honda rates the EU2200i at 8.1 hours per 0.95-gallon tank at 1/4 load, so running it around the clock for 72 hours is about 8.4 gallons, plus about nine refuels. Using it to recharge a battery bank at full output for about 1.6 hours a day burns about 0.47 gallons a day, or about 1.4 gallons for three days, based on Honda's 3.2 hours per tank at its rated 1,800 W.

Can I run a generator in the garage with the door open?

No. The CDC says never to run a generator inside a home, basement or garage, even with doors and windows open, and to keep it outside and more than 20 feet from windows, doors and vents. Ready.gov gives the same 20-foot rule and adds attached garages. Put battery or battery-backup CO detectors in the house.

Will my food be safe during a 3-day outage?

Not without power for the fridge. FoodSafety.gov says a closed refrigerator keeps food safe for about 4 hours, and perishables should be thrown out after 4 hours without power. A full freezer holds about 48 hours (24 if half full) if the door stays closed. Three days is longer than either, so the fridge is usually the biggest load worth backing up.

What about CPAP, oxygen and refrigerated medication?

Talk to your doctor or equipment supplier before an outage, as Ready.gov advises, and test your setup with the wall power off. Ready.gov also says to throw out refrigerated medication after power has been out for more than a day unless the label says otherwise, so ask your pharmacist about insulin and similar drugs. Oxygen concentrators draw far more than a CPAP and aren't covered by the numbers on this page.
