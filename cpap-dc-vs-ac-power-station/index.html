CPAP on DC vs AC from a power station

Running a CPAP from a power station's 12 V port with the maker's DC cord skips the station's inverter, so it usually lasts longer than the AC estimates on this site.

Medical safety notice: Will It Run? gives general power estimates only, not medical advice. Power draw varies by model, settings, pressure, humidifier and altitude. Before relying on any battery, power station, inverter or generator for a medical device, confirm approved power sources with the device manufacturer and your doctor or equipment supplier, and test your setup before an emergency. Never change prescribed settings to extend battery life. If your therapy is life-sustaining, keep the backup your supplier provides, register with your utility's medical-needs program, and have an evacuation plan. Run generators outdoors only, at least 20 ft from windows and doors, because of carbon monoxide risk. In an emergency, call 911.

Willrun's runtimes assume AC power and take off 15% for inverter and conversion loss (see the methodology). Manufacturers do not publish a separate DC consumption figure, so we do not print DC runtimes. Treat the AC numbers as a conservative floor.

What the manuals say

  • Philips says to use only a Philips Respironics DC power cord and battery adapter cable, and not to run the device from a vehicle battery while the engine is running (DreamStation manual).
  • The DreamStation 2 manual says its battery pack does not support humidification, and an 80 W supply is needed for the humidifier (DreamStation 2 manual).
  • Use the DC converter made for your exact model. Ask your equipment supplier.

Questions

Is DC better for a CPAP on a power station?

Usually yes, because it avoids inverter loss. Use only the manufacturer DC cord for your model.
