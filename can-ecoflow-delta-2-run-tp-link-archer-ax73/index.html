Questions

Can the EcoFlow DELTA 2 run TP-Link Archer AX73 router, networked standby?

Runs. Draw is 8.7 W, within the 1,800 W continuous output. No separate surge was published for this electronic load. Estimated runtime: about 4.2 days on a simple capacity estimate, or about 36.7 hr counting about 15 W of inverter draw.

What watts were compared?

Station continuous output is 1,800 W. No motor-surge rating is applied. Appliance running draw is 8.7 W.

How was the runtime calculated?

1,024 Wh × 0.85 = 870 Wh usable, divided by 8.7 W average draw. That is a simple estimate. On AC the station’s inverter also draws power while it is on: BLUETTI publishes about 15 W of self-consumption for the AC180, and an owner test measured about 17.5 W on the DELTA 3 Plus. Counting about 15 W, 870 Wh ÷ (8.7 W + 15 W) = about 36.7 hr. The gap matters most for small loads.

Where did the numbers come from?

US product page: about 1 kWh, 1,800 W output, X-Boost up to 2,200 W, LiFePO4, 3,000+ cycles. Weight, size, and the 4 AC outlets are from the EcoFlow DELTA 2 user manual. The manual line that pairs 1,800 W with a 2,700 W surge is printed next to 230 V, so that surge is not used for this 120 V entry. TP-Link lists typical power consumption in networked standby, with all wired ports connected and all wireless radios activated, at 8.7 W. The power adapter is 12 V, 2.5 A, which is a supply rating and is not used as the draw.
