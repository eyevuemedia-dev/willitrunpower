Danby DAR010A1BDB 1.0 cu ft compact refrigerator

Any power station with 300 W or more of AC output covers this mini fridge's 90 W running draw, so the question is hours, not watts. Danby and ENERGY STAR rate the DAR010A1BDB at 241 kWh a year, about 27.5 W on average (660 Wh a day). With about 15 W of inverter idle added, a 1 kWh station runs it about 21 hours and a 2 kWh station about 41 hours. Danby doesn't publish start-up watts, so test it once before you rely on it.

Short answer

  • Watts are easy: 90 W running (Danby). Every station we list has at least 300 W continuous.
  • Hours depend on inverter idle: the fridge averages 27.5 W, and the inverter adds about 15 W, which is 35% of the total. A Jackery Explorer 1000 v2 runs it about 21 hours; without the idle it would be about 33.
  • Small stations: about 5 hours on a 256 Wh EcoFlow RIVER 2 and 6 hours on a 288 Wh Anker SOLIX C300: one evening, not one night and day.
  • For medicine or long outages, a 12 V compressor cooler on the station's car socket uses about 11 W with no inverter, so the same 1 kWh station lasts about 82 hours. See our insulin fridge guide.
  • Not for vans or RVs: Danby's manual says this fridge isn't designed for vehicles, garages or other spaces that aren't temperature-controlled.

How efficient is it?

Among the 8 ENERGY STAR records (some are color variants of one model) for 0.9–1.1 cu ft automatic-defrost compact fridges, the DAR010A1BDB's 241 kWh is near the top (range 106–242, median 174.5). Danby's newer DAR010A2BDB, certified 2026-11-11, is rated 187 kWh, 22% less, which would add about 4 hours on a 1 kWh station. And the median 3.0–3.3 cu ft compact (255 kWh) uses only about 6% more than this 1.0 cu ft model, so if you have the space, more fridge costs very little extra battery.

Why inverter idle matters more than the fridge here

A station's inverter draws power whenever its AC outlets are on, whether or not the compressor is running. We use about 15 W, the figure BLUETTI builds into its AC180 runtime formula (see methodology). For a 27.5 W average load, that idle is 35% of everything the station delivers. Over 24 hours it is 360 Wh, more than half of the fridge's own 660 Wh.

Two practical takeaways: if your station has an AC timeout or eco auto-off setting, turn it off for a fridge, because the outlet can switch off while the compressor rests between cycles; and if the fridge is the only AC load, the efficient fix is a DC cooler, not a bigger battery.

Recharging from solar

One day on AC is (660 Wh + 360 Wh idle) ÷ (sun hours × 0.75): about 304 W of panels on a Michigan annual average (4.47 h), 217 W in a Detroit July (6.28 h) and 627 W in a Detroit December (2.17 h), using NREL PVWatts sun hours (PVWatts). The 12 V cooler needs about 163 W in a Detroit December. That gap is the inverter idle again.

Alternatives

  • 12 V compressor cooler (for example the Dometic CFX3 25, about 11 W in a 90°F room): no inverter, works in vehicles, which Danby says this fridge is not designed for. Our insulin fridge guide compares them in detail.
  • A larger compact fridge if you need space: the median 3.0–3.3 cu ft model uses only slightly more energy (chart above).
  • Cooler and ice for a short outage: USDA FSIS gives about 4 hours for a closed refrigerator; a small fridge may warm faster, so keep a thermometer inside and go by 40°F.
  • A computer UPS is the wrong tool: most are sized for minutes of runtime, not hours.

Moving the plug: when you switch the fridge from the wall to the station (or back), Danby says to wait 3 to 5 minutes before restarting it after an interruption.

Questions

Can a power station run a Danby DAR010A1BDB?

Yes. Danby lists 90 W running, and every station we cover has at least 300 W of continuous AC output. Danby doesn't publish start-up watts, so run it on the station once on a normal day before you depend on it.

How long will a power station run a Danby mini fridge?

About 21 hours on a 1 kWh station and 41 hours on a 2 kWh station, using ENERGY STAR's 241 kWh a year (27.5 W average) plus about 15 W of inverter idle and 85% usable capacity.

How many watts does the Danby DAR010A1BDB use?

90 W while the compressor runs, and about 27.5 W averaged over a year (241 kWh, 660 Wh a day), because the compressor runs roughly 31% of the time.

Why does my station run the mini fridge for fewer hours than expected?

Inverter idle. With the AC outlets on, the station uses about 15 W even when the compressor is off, which is 35% of the total for this fridge. A 12 V cooler on the car socket avoids it.

Can I use the DAR010A1BDB in an RV or van?

Danby's manual says it is not designed for vehicles, garages or other spaces that aren't temperature-controlled. A 12 V compressor cooler is built for that job.

Is there a more efficient version?

Yes. Danby's DAR010A2BDB, ENERGY STAR-certified in November 2026, is rated 187 kWh a year versus 241 for the DAR010A1BDB.
