Frigidaire FFFH20F3W 20 cu ft upright freezer

You may not need a battery for this freezer at all on day one: USDA FSIS says a full freezer holds its temperature about 48 hours with the door shut. For longer outages, size by energy. ENERGY STAR rates the FFFH20F3W at 478 kWh a year (about 54.6 W average, 1,310 Wh a day), so a 2 kWh station with a published motor surge runs it about 25 hours. Frigidaire publishes 115 V and a 15 A minimum circuit but no running or start-up watts, so we don't give a pass/fail verdict per station; our rule is a published motor surge of at least 1,200 W.

Short answer

  • Outage under a day, freezer full: keep the door shut and spend your battery on the fridge. FSIS: full freezer about 48 hours, half full about 24.
  • Two to three days: a 2 kWh station with a published motor surge, such as the EcoFlow DELTA 2 Max, runs this freezer about 25 hours. Start it once the freezer has coasted for a day, and one charge covers day two.
  • Fridge and freezer together: about 2,665 Wh a day with a median top-freezer, so a 2 kWh station lasts about 16 hours. Pick a large surge (the BLUETTI AC200L lists 7,200 W) so both compressors can start at once, or use a generator.
  • No station: FSIS says 50 lb of dry ice keeps a full 18 cu ft freezer cold for two days. Scaled to this freezer's 20 cu ft, that is roughly 56 lb (our scaling, not an FSIS figure).

Will a station start it?

Frigidaire doesn't publish the compressor's running or start-up watts for the FFFH20F3W. Its 15 A minimum circuit is a wiring requirement, not a draw figure. The closest published number is the predecessor FFFH20F2QW's 0.60 kW connected load at 115 V, a nameplate-style rating for the whole appliance. Every station below has at least 1,000 W continuous, so continuous output isn't the limit.

For start-up we use the same rule as our refrigerator guide: Haier publishes 805 W at start-up vs 195.5 W running for its 18 and 21 cu ft refrigerators (Haier), and Champion's chart plans 800–1,200 W starting for a refrigerator (Champion). A station needs a maker-published surge of about 1,200 W that applies to motors; boost modes don't count. Plug the freezer straight into the station's AC outlet rather than through a power strip.

Frost-free freezers run a defrost heater on a timer. Its energy is already inside the ENERGY STAR annual figure, but Frigidaire doesn't publish the heater's watts, so don't plan to run the battery to empty; keep some charge in reserve.

The 48-hour head start, and how to use it

A freezer is the easiest load in an outage to postpone. USDA FSIS: a full freezer holds its temperature about 48 hours and a half-full one about 24 hours if the door stays shut (USDA FSIS). That changes the plan:

  1. Hour 0–24: leave the freezer unplugged and closed. Give the station to the fridge (which only holds about 4 hours), the router and the lights.
  2. Check the thermometer, not the clock. FSIS says 0°F or lower is the freezer target. The FFFH20F3W's high-temperature alarm also tells you when it has warmed. A half-empty freezer gets there much sooner.
  3. Day 2–3: run the freezer from the station for a few hours at a time to pull it back down, then let it coast again, or run it continuously if the station has the capacity.
  4. After the outage: FSIS says thawed food can be refrozen if it still has ice crystals or is at 40°F or below. When in doubt, throw it out.

Before a forecast storm, fill empty space with frozen water jugs (FSIS suggests freezing water in bags or containers and grouping food together); a full freezer coasts twice as long as a half-full one.

Nameplate capacity = (daily Wh + 360 Wh of inverter idle) × days ÷ 0.85. ENERGY STAR figures come from a DOE lab test; a warm room, frequent door openings and a full load of warm groceries push real use up, so leave headroom.

Because the freezer can coast for the first day, the realistic 3-day battery plan for fridge + freezer is the fridge for three days plus the freezer for two: about 7,864 Wh of nameplate, nearly twice the largest single station here, the EcoFlow DELTA Pro 3 (4,096 Wh), so you need expansion batteries or a way to recharge. That is why most fridge-plus-freezer homes end up with a generator recharge; see the 3-day plan.

Recharging from solar

To replace one day of this freezer from panels: (1,310 Wh + 360 Wh inverter idle) ÷ (sun hours × 0.75), about 498 W of panels on a Michigan annual average, 354 W in a Detroit July and 1,026 W in a Detroit December (NREL PVWatts). December is beyond the 1,000 W solar input of the DELTA 2 Max and close to the AC200L's 1,200 W; only the DELTA Pro 3 (2,600 W) and F3800 (2,400 W) have real margin. In the north, plan winter freezer backup around a generator and the 48-hour coast, not solar.

Alternatives to a big battery

  • Dry ice. FSIS: 50 lb keeps a fully stocked 18 cu ft freezer cold for two days. Handle it with gloves, don't put it directly on food, and ventilate the room.
  • Small inverter generator. A 2,000 W-class inverter such as the Honda EU2200i (1,800 W running, 2,200 W starting) can run a fridge and this freezer if they don't start at the same moment (see what a 2,000 W generator runs). Outdoors only, 20 ft from openings (CDC).
  • Generator plus station: run the generator an hour or two a day to recharge the station and pull the freezer down, then shut it off. It uses a fraction of the fuel of running all night (generator vs power station).
  • A station without a published motor surge may start it, but the maker doesn't say so. Test it on a normal day first.

What we'd buy for this freezer

Questions

Can a power station run a Frigidaire FFFH20F3W freezer?

Yes, if it has at least 1,000 W continuous and a maker-published motor surge of about 1,200 W or more. Frigidaire doesn't publish running or start-up watts for this model, so we use Haier's published 805 W start-up and Champion's 800–1,200 W refrigerator range as the guide. Test it on a normal day first.

How long will a power station run a 20 cu ft upright freezer?

For the FFFH20F3W, about 13 hours on a 1 kWh station and about 25 hours on a 2 kWh station, using ENERGY STAR's 478 kWh a year (54.6 W average) plus about 15 W of inverter idle and 85% usable capacity.

How many watts does the Frigidaire FFFH20F3W use?

ENERGY STAR rates it at 478 kWh a year, about 1,310 Wh a day or 54.6 W on average. Frigidaire publishes 115 V and a 15 A minimum circuit, but not running or start-up watts.

How long does a full freezer stay frozen without power?

USDA FSIS says about 48 hours for a full freezer and about 24 hours for a half-full one, if the door stays closed. That is why the fridge should get the battery first.

Should I run my fridge or my freezer on a power station first?

The fridge. It only holds food safely about 4 hours with no power, while a full freezer holds about 48. Bring the freezer onto the station on day two, or when its thermometer or high-temperature alarm says it has warmed.

How much dry ice do I need for a 20 cu ft freezer?

FSIS says 50 lb keeps a full 18 cu ft freezer cold for two days. Scaling that by volume gives roughly 56 lb for 20 cu ft. That is our estimate, not an FSIS figure; a half-empty freezer will need more.
