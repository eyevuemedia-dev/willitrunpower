Whirlpool WZC3209LW 9 cu ft chest freezer

Size a power station for this chest freezer by energy: ENERGY STAR and the EnergyGuide label rate the WZC3209LW at 201 kWh a year, about 551 Wh a day or 22.9 W on average, the lowest figure in its Canadian comparison group (201–227 kWh). That is little enough that a 1 kWh station with a published motor surge runs it about 24 hours, and a 2 kWh station about 1.9 days. Whirlpool publishes 110 V / 3.5 A but no start-up watts, so we don't give a per-station pass/fail; our rule is a maker-published motor surge of at least 1,200 W.

Short answer

  • One overnight outage: you may not need to power it at all. Whirlpool says to keep the lid closed if the power will be out 24 hours or less, and USDA FSIS says a full freezer holds about 48 hours (24 if half full).
  • Two days or more: a 1 kWh station with a published motor surge, such as the Anker SOLIX C1000 or Jackery Explorer 1000 v2, runs it about 24 hours per charge. At 22.9 W average it draws less than any refrigerator or freezer in our appliance records; inverter idle is about 40% of the total.
  • A week: a 2 kWh station like the EcoFlow DELTA 2 Max (1.9 days per charge) plus 400–600 W of solar, or a small generator run an hour or two a day.
  • Freezer plus your fridge: the freezer is the easy part. A top-freezer fridge like the Samsung RT18DG6300 uses 1.7× as much energy.

ENERGY STAR and EnergyGuide figures come from the DOE lab test. A hot garage, frequent lid openings or a load of unfrozen food push real use up, so leave headroom.

The 3.5 A figure is the most-quoted number and the least useful one. It is the rated current for the whole appliance, so it sets a ceiling of about 385 W. The compressor's real running draw on a freezer this size is lower, and the number that matters for a power station, the start-up surge, isn't published at all. That is why we use the energy figure for runtime and a published-surge rule for "will it start".

Will a power station start it?

Whirlpool doesn't publish start-up watts. Haier publishes 805 W of start-up for a refrigerator, and Champion lists 800–1,200 W of starting watts for a refrigerator. A 9 cu ft chest freezer has a smaller compressor than a full-size fridge, but we still apply the same rule: at least 1,000 W continuous and a maker-published motor surge of 1,200 W or more. Stations without a published surge may well start it; test it on a normal day first.

How it compares with other fridges and freezers

Two things stand out. A chest freezer loses less cold air when opened because cold air sinks, and this one uses 58% less energy than the 20 cu ft upright Frigidaire FFFH20F3W (478 kWh). It even beats the 1 cu ft Danby DAR010A1BDB mini fridge (241 kWh). If you are choosing a freezer with outages in mind, small and chest-style is the battery-friendly choice.

One caveat: in the ENERGY STAR list we downloaded, this is the only certified chest freezer between 7.5 and 9.4 cu ft, so there is no larger peer set to rank it against. The Canadian EnerGuide label, which compares all models of that size, puts it at the low end (201 vs 227 kWh).

The 48-hour coast: when you don't need to power it

A freezer is the one appliance that can wait. Whirlpool's manual: "If the power will be out for 24 hours or less, keep the door closed to help food stay cold and frozen." For longer outages it says to move food to a frozen-food locker or use it, and notes that food with ice crystals can be refrozen. USDA FSIS gives about 48 hours for a full freezer and 24 hours for a half-full one (FSIS).

  • Day 1: lid closed, battery goes to the fridge, router and lights. A fridge holds food only about 4 hours.
  • Day 2: put the freezer on the station, or on a generator for a few hours, before it passes 0°F. An inexpensive freezer thermometer tells you when; the red LED on this model only lights when the cabinet is too warm.
  • Restarting: after a long outage, Whirlpool says the compressor runs continuously until the cabinet is cold again (6–8 hours from warm). Expect higher draw than the 23 W average for those first hours.
  • Fill empty space with frozen water jugs. Whirlpool and FSIS both note that a full freezer holds longer, and the jugs double as cooler ice for the fridge food.

Fridge mode: the WZC3209LW can be switched to a 33.8–41°F refrigerator with the thermostat. Whirlpool says to empty and defrost it first, so it isn't a mid-outage trick. It is useful as a second fridge that can live in a garage, but Whirlpool doesn't publish energy use in fridge mode, so we don't estimate it.

Recharging from solar

One day of this freezer on a station is (551 Wh + 360 Wh inverter idle) ÷ (sun hours × 0.75). With NREL PVWatts sun hours (PVWatts): about 272 W of panels on a Michigan annual average (4.47 h), 193 W in a Detroit July (6.28 h) and 560 W in a Detroit December (2.17 h). Most 1 kWh stations accept 400–600 W of solar, so a single station plus two or three 200 W panels can keep this freezer going indefinitely in summer and nearly so in winter, unless snow covers the panels.

Alternatives to a big battery

  • Small inverter generator, a few hours a day. A 2,000 W-class unit such as the Honda EU2200i (1,800 W running, 2,200 W starting) covers Champion's 800–1,200 W refrigerator range. Run it outdoors, at least 20 ft from windows and doors (CDC). Because the freezer coasts so well, two or three hours of generator time a day, with a thermometer inside, is a realistic plan; see generator vs power station.
  • Dry ice. FSIS says 50 lb of dry ice keeps a full 18 cu ft freezer cold for two days; a 9 cu ft chest needs about half that by volume (our scaling, not an FSIS figure). Handle it with gloves and keep the room ventilated.
  • A station you already own, even a small one without a published surge: test it on a normal day, then run the freezer in blocks.

What we'd buy for this freezer

  • Freezer only, least money: Anker SOLIX C1000 (1,056 Wh, 1,800 W, 2,400 W surge, 600 W solar input): about 24 hours per charge.
  • Freezer for a week with panels: EcoFlow DELTA 2 Max (2,048 Wh, 4,800 W surge, 1,000 W solar): about 1.9 days per charge, and winter solar headroom.
  • No station: a full freezer, frozen jugs, a thermometer and a plan for day 2 (generator or dry ice). For an outage under 24 hours that is all Whirlpool says you need.

Questions

Can a power station run a Whirlpool WZC3209LW chest freezer?

Yes, if it has at least 1,000 W continuous and a maker-published motor surge of about 1,200 W or more. Whirlpool publishes 110 V / 3.5 A but no start-up watts, so we use Champion's 800–1,200 W refrigerator starting range as the guide. Test it on a normal day first.

How long will a power station run the WZC3209LW?

About 24 hours on a 1 kWh station and about 1.9 days on a 2 kWh station, using ENERGY STAR's 201 kWh a year (22.9 W average) plus about 15 W of inverter idle and 85% usable capacity.

How many watts does the Whirlpool WZC3209LW use?

About 22.9 W averaged over a year (201 kWh, 551 Wh a day). Its rated current is 3.5 A at 110 V, about 385 W for the whole appliance, which is a ceiling, not running watts.

Do I need to power a chest freezer during a short outage?

Often not. Whirlpool says to keep the lid closed if the power will be out 24 hours or less, and USDA FSIS says a full freezer holds about 48 hours. Put it on a station or generator from day two, or when a thermometer shows it warming toward 0°F.

Can the WZC3209LW be used as a refrigerator?

Yes. Whirlpool's manual lists a refrigerator setting of 33.8–41°F. Empty and defrost it before switching. Whirlpool doesn't publish energy use in fridge mode.

Can I keep the WZC3209LW in a garage?

Whirlpool lists a 32°F to 110°F room range, so a garage that stays in that range is fine. Below freezing it may not run enough to keep food frozen; above 110°F it is outside the rating.
