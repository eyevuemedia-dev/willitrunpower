Samsung RT18DG6300 17.5 cu ft top-freezer refrigerator

Size a power station for this fridge by energy, not watts. ENERGY STAR rates the RT18DG6300 at 333 kWh a year (about 38 W on average, 912 Wh a day), the lowest figure among 350 certified 16.5–18.5 cu ft top-freezers. A 1 kWh station with a published motor surge runs it about 17 hours, and a 2 kWh station about 33 hours, counting inverter idle. Samsung prints 115 V / 3.5 A on the spec sheet but no start-up watts, so we don't give a pass/fail verdict per station; our rule is a published motor surge of at least 1,200 W.

Short answer

  • Overnight outage: a 1 kWh station with a published motor surge, such as the Anker SOLIX C1000 (2,400 W surge), covers about 17 hours.
  • A full day or more: a 2 kWh station such as the Jackery Explorer 2000 v2 or EcoFlow DELTA 2 Max runs it about 33 hours, more than a day on one charge. That is about two hours more than a median top-freezer of the same size gets.
  • Three days: about 4,491 Wh of nameplate capacity. No single station in our data except the EcoFlow DELTA Pro 3 (2.7 days) gets close without recharging, so plan on solar, an expansion battery or a generator.
  • Keep the door shut: a closed fridge holds food safely for about 4 hours with no power (USDA FSIS), so you can also run it in blocks.

What 3.5 A means for a power station

3.5 A × 115 V is about 402 W. That is the rated current for the whole appliance, not what the compressor draws while it runs, and it isn't a start-up figure. Every station that passes our surge rule has at least 1,000 W continuous, more than twice that rating, so continuous output isn't the limit.

The open question is the start-up surge, and Samsung doesn't publish one. Samsung describes the compressor as variable speed, but we haven't found a Samsung start-up figure, so we use the same rule as our refrigerator guide: Haier publishes 805 W at start-up vs 195.5 W running for its 18 and 21 cu ft refrigerators (Haier), and Champion's generator chart plans 800–1,200 W starting for a refrigerator (Champion). A station needs a maker-published surge of about 1,200 W that applies to motors. EcoFlow X-Boost and BLUETTI Power Lifting don't count.

On a station, plug the fridge straight into an AC outlet with its own cord, not through a power strip or a long extension cord, and keep other big loads off while the compressor starts.

Stretching one charge without risking the food

  • Keep the doors closed. USDA FSIS says a closed refrigerator keeps food cold for about 4 hours, and a full freezer holds its temperature about 48 hours (24 if half full) (USDA FSIS).
  • Run it in blocks if the battery is small. Because the fridge can coast about 4 hours, a short-capacity station can run it for a few hours, then rest. Keep an appliance thermometer inside: FSIS says 40°F or lower in the fridge and 0°F or lower in the freezer, and perishables above 40°F for 2 hours or more should be discarded.
  • Leave Power Cool and Power Freeze off. Samsung lists both as features that speed up cooling; on a battery you want the compressor's normal cycle, not a boost.
  • Pre-chill before a forecast storm. FSIS suggests freezing water in quart bags or containers to pack around food. The 4.9 cu ft freezer section fills quickly, so a few frozen bottles in the fridge section help most.
  • Test it once on a normal day. Plug the fridge into the station for 24 hours and note the percentage used. That number beats any table, including ours, and it tells you if the start-up surge is a problem.

Recharging from solar

To replace one day of this fridge from panels: (912 Wh + 360 Wh inverter idle) ÷ (sun hours × 0.75). With NREL PVWatts sun hours (PVWatts), that is about 380 W of panels on a Michigan annual average (4.47 h), 270 W in a Detroit July (6.28 h) and 782 W in a Detroit December (2.17 h). The Jackery Explorer 2000 v2 accepts 400 W of solar, so it can't keep up in winter; the DELTA 2 Max (1,000 W) and AC200L (1,200 W) can. On paper, a station with 1,000 W of solar input keeps up with this fridge even in a Detroit December; a median bottom-freezer needs about 1,126 W there. Snow on the panels takes output to zero.

Alternatives to a big battery

  • Small inverter generator. A 2,000 W-class inverter such as the Honda EU2200i (1,800 W running, 2,200 W starting) covers Champion's 800–1,200 W refrigerator start range. It must run outdoors, at least 20 ft from windows and doors (CDC). The most fuel-efficient way to use it is to recharge a station for an hour or two a day; see generator vs power station.
  • Coolers and ice. For an outage you expect to last a few hours past the 4-hour mark, FSIS suggests moving perishables to coolers with ice and keeping block or dry ice on hand.
  • Run it in blocks from a smaller station you already own, using the thermometer rule above.
  • A station without a published motor surge (for example the EcoFlow DELTA 2, BLUETTI AC180 or Anker SOLIX C800) may well start this fridge, but the maker doesn't say it will. Test it on a normal day before you count on it.

Questions

Can a power station run a Samsung RT18DG6300?

Yes, if it has at least 1,000 W continuous and a maker-published motor surge of about 1,200 W or more. Samsung publishes 115 V / 3.5 A but no start-up watts, so we use Haier's published 805 W start-up and Champion's 800–1,200 W refrigerator range as the guide. Test it on a normal day before relying on it.

How long will a power station run a Samsung RT18DG6300?

About 17 hours on a 1 kWh station and about 33 hours on a 2 kWh station, using ENERGY STAR's 333 kWh a year (38 W average) plus about 15 W of inverter idle and 85% usable capacity.

How many watts does the Samsung RT18DG6300 use?

ENERGY STAR rates it at 333 kWh a year, about 912 Wh a day or 38 W on average. Samsung's spec sheet rates it 115 V / 3.5 A, about 402 W for the whole appliance; that is a nameplate rating, not running watts.

What size power station do I need for a 3-day outage with this fridge?

About 4,491 Wh of nameplate capacity for the fridge alone, counting inverter idle. That means a 4 kWh-class station, an expansion battery, or a 2 kWh station recharged by solar or a generator.

Is the RT18DG6300 efficient compared with other top-freezers?

Yes. At 333 kWh a year it has the lowest rating among 350 ENERGY STAR-certified top-freezers between 16.5 and 18.5 cu ft (median 366), 17% under the federal limit for its class.

How long is food safe in the fridge without power?

USDA FSIS says about 4 hours with the door closed. Keep a thermometer inside; discard perishables that have been above 40°F for 2 hours or more.
